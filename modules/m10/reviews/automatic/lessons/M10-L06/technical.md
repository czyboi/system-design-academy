## Verified mechanisms, arithmetic and scope

- Raw value memory is 409,600,000 bytes = 390.625 MiB, reasonably presented as about 391 MiB. Two copies require 819,200,000 bytes ≈781.25 MiB; the about-782 estimate is the stated rounded per-copy estimate doubled, before overhead. Average 10 million/day ≈115.74/s and 20× peak ≈2,315/s. At 90% hits origin fill demand is 231.5/s. With half cold and half retaining 90% hits, overall hit fraction is 45%, so demand is 1,273.25/s before control/coalescing. None is a vendor benchmark.
- Under the additional assumption that misses are distinct/uncoalesced and the origin can safely complete only 400/s, ideal immediate served capacity would be at most `0.45×2,315+400 = 1,441.75/s` (about 62.3% of originals), even before latency failures. This is a conditional illustration of T2, not a newly established workload fact or a bound when coalescing changes demand. Successful origin protection therefore cannot by itself certify 99% user success.
- Full result keys name query/filter/sort/page, authorized scope, rank version and index generation; the prose explicitly requires **all** result-changing inputs, including its locale assumption. Complete/partial result classes are separated, delayed `g17` cannot fill a current `g18` key, and historical-generation reads require a distinct contract. Serving checks, source-derived deadlines, immutable versions and bounded clock error are application obligations. T1 is the separate restriction boundary, not an objection to generation-scoped caching.
- Misses pin the index generation and check completeness/source cut. Accepted M08-L03 further requires the generation and expected logical shard set across query/fetch/retries and query-visible readiness on every eligible reader before cutover. A durable applied checkpoint alone is not that proof. This lesson's reference to the accepted source-cut contract does not create a new Redis/AWS guarantee.
- Independent full caches duplicate memory; replicated caches can retain stale data; sharding creates cold-owner/hot-key/routing-version costs. The recovery plan bounds origin concurrency, single-flight, warmup and original deadlines rather than fabricating a complete response. A hot key and the policy/generation authority remain distinct capacity concerns.
- [Redis eviction documentation, Approximated LRU](https://redis.io/docs/latest/develop/reference/eviction/#approximated-lru-algorithm) independently supports its sampled LRU distinction. [AWS Memcached lazy loading](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonElastiCache/latest/dg/Strategies.html#Strategies.LazyLoading) supports the product-example miss/fill and cold-node behavior, not the arithmetic or application coherence contract. [RFC 9111 §§4.2 and 4.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-4.2) supports HTTP age/freshness and traversed-cache invalidation; it does not enforce global application purge or authorization. All were opened independently.
