## Independently verified technical scope

The UTC half-open interval excludes its upper boundary; hourly publication and a weekly aggregation window are separate dimensions. The chosen multi-category allocation yields pA 7, pB 5, pC 4 before the return; Kitchen 12, Gifts 5, Stationery 4 sum to 21 versus unique product units 16. After the independent −3 return, pA is 4, Kitchen is 9, and Kitchen's order is pB 5 then pA 4. The sample's intentional s2 duplicate must be removed across partitions, while map/reduce attempt selection protects a different boundary.

The two jobs correctly separate pair aggregation and composite ordering. Global ordering requires matching range boundaries and ordered files, or a final reducer, rather than concatenating arbitrary hash partitions. These mechanisms are supported by [Dean and Ghemawat, MapReduce §§2.1–2.3, 3.1, 3.3, 4.1–4.3 and 5.3](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/osdi04/tech/full_papers/dean/dean_html/). Historical MapReduce promises do not supply the application's revisioned source, category policy, serving pointer or cache consistency.

The immutable-row/manifest approach avoids deliberately exposing half a result; pinning a revision across pagination and cache fill prevents old data being inserted under the new result's key. Those sound mechanisms still need T2's explicit eligibility rule. The all-original-valid-read denominator correctly retains rejected, failed, late, wrong-revision and incorrect-ranking outcomes, with admitted-only speed insufficient for success. The stated 99%, five-minute cohort and 300 ms target are synthetic thresholds. No numeric batch-capacity guarantee is claimed by the daily traffic figures alone.

I opened the original MapReduce publication and full paper, current Beam guide, Flink windows page (displaying **v2.3.0**), and official Dataflow streaming-pipeline page. Beam/Dataflow describe windows, late input and triggers; they do not give this scenario an atomic external ranking publication. Flink's mutable `flink-docs-stable` URL currently resolves to the named version; the draft makes no stronger cross-version guarantee. These contextual references are valid but do not resolve application-specific T1–T4 by themselves.
