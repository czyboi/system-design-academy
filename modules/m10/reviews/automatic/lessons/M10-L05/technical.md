- The canonical seven-edge graph yields BFS layers `{a}`, `{b,c}`, `{d,e}`, `{f}`, `{g}` and two four-edge routes. Sorted exploration chooses the stated parent chain; the diagram's explicit edge list removes its visual ambiguity. Query-local visited-on-enqueue and predecessor storage preserve one-sided BFS correctness for the stated static, unweighted visible graph. The shared global visited failure is real. The [NetworkX source](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/_modules/networkx/algorithms/shortest_paths/unweighted.html#single_source_shortest_path) independently shows per-call BFS state and layer traversal; it is corroboration, not code imported into the lesson.
- Directed backward search needs incoming adjacency; the checked implementation uses predecessor versus successor views. `O(V+E)` is appropriately scoped to adjacency-list exploration. The `b^d`/`2b^(d/2)` comparison is explicitly a tree-like approximation and not a network latency bound. T1 concerns the missing distributed stop certificate, not those distinctions.
- 2 billion undirected edges imply about 4 billion endpoint references; eight bytes each gives 32 billion bytes, and mean degree is 40 over 100 million vertices. Metadata, replication and skew are excluded explicitly. The P1/P2/P3 owner batches match the example. Timeout is not an empty list, and paginated high-degree adjacency must carry completeness before an exact result. Snapshot and routing identities are required application contracts, not attributed to arbitrary stores.
- The graph revision, viewer scope and final path-edge authorization are distinguished from an endpoint-only cache. Revocation during execution requires the chosen protected validation boundary to enforce its policy; an old scoped cache alone cannot certify current authorization. Approximate pruning is correctly separated from exact shortest path. The final safety tests ask for authority evidence rather than controller logs.
- [Neo4j shortest-path planning](https://neo4j.com/docs/cypher-manual/current/patterns/shortest-paths/#performance-and-planning) supports its suitable-query bidirectional behavior and whole-path-filter caveat. [GraphQL Foundation's introduction](https://graphql.org/learn/introduction/) confirms the API/runtime and storage independence; [Apache Gremlin](https://tinkerpop.apache.org/gremlin.html) supports its traversal-language role. Neither is a universal shortest-path or authorization guarantee. [TAO's original publisher record](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc13/technical-sessions/presentation/bronson) is correctly limited to a historical social-association datastore rather than an exact-path service.
