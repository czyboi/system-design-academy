# M10-L04 companion — Financial aggregation and budgeting

## Six checks and explained answers

**Q1 (LO1).** A worker fetched two cursor pages from a transaction API, then crashed before persisting its changes. Can it advance the stored cursor to the final page merely because those HTTP calls returned successfully?

**Answer.** No. Durable cursor progress must not pass unapplied changes. Replay from the last committed cursor with stable source identities, apply a coherent update batch with cursor advancement under the provider's contract, then publish downstream change intent. An HTTP response is not proof the local ledger reflects it.

**Q2 (LO1).** A pending transaction `p7` for 40 USD is removed and a posted `q9` for 40 USD appears. Both have unique IDs. Why can a naïve `SUM(amount)` still be wrong?

**Answer.** The two records may represent one economic purchase at different lifecycle stages. Reconcile pending-to-posted linkage and maintain one selected canonical current representation in the spending view. Raw audit can retain both transitions without counting both as purchases.

**Q3 (LO1).** For January, posted `t1=+75`, `t2=+40`, `t3=-20`, `t4=+60`, with t1 and t3 and t4 in Groceries, t2 in Transport, and `t1` is replayed. What are the totals?

**Answer.** Groceries 115 USD and Transport 40 USD. Deduplicate by stable source transaction identity, not by counting fetch events. Use integer minor units and the same period/currency.

**Q4 (LO2).** A user reclassifies `t4` (60 USD) from Groceries to Transport. What happens to totals and budget alerts?

**Answer.** New totals Groceries 55, Transport 100. Under sample limits 100 and 80, retract or correct the prior Groceries exceedance and consider a new Transport exceedance by 20. Carry a higher classification and aggregate revision, and version alert transitions so delayed messages can't present the old result as current.

**Q5 (LO2).** Is the hash of a banking password a reliable cross-connection account ID? Should refresh tokens or full transaction descriptions be put on the warehouse queue?

**Answer.** No. Passwords rotate, can be shared, and may never be exposed through delegated integration; use provider-documented account identity with tenant scope and consent. Tokens are secrets; avoid copying them into analytics or event logs, and minimize/redact personal fields.

**Q6 (LO2).** Can a warehouse result generated 30 minutes ago be called the user's current bank balance if a webhook was received five minutes ago?

**Answer.** No. Webhook is a trigger, not proof of completed reconciliation or a bank-authoritative current balance. Show as-of/source-cut timestamp and meaningful freshness/error state. OLTP and warehouse have different latency and contractual purposes.

## EX-1 — Replayed refresh and changed budget category

**Scenario.** A consenting user has USD posted transactions t1 Groceries +75, t2 Transport +40, t3 Groceries refund -20, t4 Groceries +60. January Groceries budget is 100 USD and Transport 80. Two manual refresh attempts use the same original key. A provider emits t1 twice. Separately, for February, pending `p7` of 40 USD is removed when posted `q9` of 40 USD in Transport appears; this lifecycle trace is outside the January calculation. Count q9 once in February, not once under each ID. After the initial monthly report the user changes t4 to Transport. A stale classifier tries to publish an earlier version and two alert-delivery attempts race. For an illustrative five-minute ingress cohort, 1,000 original authorized refreshes have distinct original IDs; retries reuse those IDs. Each has 15 minutes from first ingress to a managed-client-confirmed, published reconciled view. Initial links require complete requested provider-supported history with no known gap. Following each original through its deadline and later completion where applicable yields exclusive outcomes: 970 useful complete views on time, 10 still partial initial views at deadline, 8 complete only after their deadlines, 7 rejected/throttled and 5 failed or unknown. The target is 99% useful complete views on time; these numbers are synthetic.

**Deliverables.** Define account consent/token/identity boundaries; draw durable refresh and source change flow through a cursor/canonical transaction store, protected raw log, current aggregate and notification; calculate initial/final totals and active alerts; show why posted q9 must replace p7 rather than add to it twice; justify the OLTP, warehouse and object-store split, and calculate the original-trigger SLI and decide whether it meets the target; separate admitted-only latency and category accuracy from that decision. Include completion versus budget notifications and at least two crash/race tests, including a successful empty initial response.

### Worked reference

1. Link with supported scope/consent and restricted provider credentials. Never use password hashes as account IDs; only provider-supported scoped identities. Durable intent claims the original manual key before dispatch; a second same-key request returns the same operation state. Store no provider token in budget/warehouse events.
2. Sync from committed cursor C0 and stage provider added/modified/removed pages. Claim app generation G for the linked Item; commit current transaction identities and cursor C1 together only if G and expected C0 still match the current authority, then emit a recoverable change event. An old run cannot apply or publish after a newer generation commits. Cursors are opaque continuation tokens, not sortable revisions; the application generation and category revision are distinct. A crash before commit resumes from C0; a duplicate t1 is an update/no-op for one source ID. A pending-to-posted transition replaces the pending representation; q9 contributes 40 USD once to February Transport after posting and contributes nothing to January. On first link, a successful empty `NOT_READY` response stays initializing; initial data can be shown as partial. Only after historical readiness for the requested supported range, no known gap, coherent application and cursor/view commit can the initial view count as complete. Unsupported or unverified history remains a visible gap.
3. January initial `Groceries=75-20+60=115` and `Transport=40` (USD), so Groceries is 15 over its exercise budget. After category override for t4, `Groceries=55`, `Transport=100`, and Transport is 20 over. The new version's committed totals supersede the old; stale classifier output must fail a version/cut comparison.
4. A refresh completion intent is versioned/idempotent by original refresh and committed sync generation, and is queued only after required coverage, cursor and coherent view commit; partial or failed sync is not a completion. A separate budget alert intent follows the aggregate revision; delivery dedup key includes threshold transition and budget revision. Replaying a “Groceries exceeded” alert after the revision is obsolete and should be suppressed or corrected by policy. No notifications mutate the ledger.
5. OLTP stores live accounts, source IDs, category rules, recent summary; protected object log supports replay/retention; warehouse serves large historical groupings with as-of timestamps. Separate currency domains; do not incorrectly sum USD with other units.
6. The five-minute cohort denominator is 1,000 original authorized refreshes, not retry attempts. The numerator is 970 managed-client-confirmed complete reconciled views within each original’s 15-minute first-ingress deadline, including required supported initial coverage. Thus **970/1,000 = 97%**, below 99%; partial, late, rejected/throttled and failed/unknown outcomes remain unsuccessful. Admitted-only latency cannot establish the SLO; measure classification accuracy separately. Tests: successful empty `NOT_READY` then partial and historical completion without an early completion notice; crash after the last page before cursor commit; an older fenced generation trying to commit after a newer one; pending-to-posted swap; late classifier and duplicate notification delivery.
