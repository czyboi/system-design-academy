# M10-L02 assessment — Social feed publication and timelines

## Six checks

**Q1 (LO1):** A post row commits but a crash occurs before a separate fan-out enqueue. The API already replied “published.” What invariant is missing?

**Answer.** A durable runnable event intent needs to be committed with the post, or a proven recovery scanner must rediscover the committed post. Otherwise a durable author post can remain permanently absent from home timelines. A queue acknowledgment alone does not imply recipients were updated.

**Q2 (LO1):** Why can Ada's user timeline show `a10` before Ben's home timeline does, even though `POST /posts` succeeded?

**Answer.** The author timeline reads the canonical committed post by author. Ben's home inbox is a delayed projection fed by an asynchronous worker; its freshness has a distinct target and error signals.

**Q3 (LO1):** A fan-out worker retries after a timeout and appends the same `post_id` for one recipient again. What must protect the inbox, and what if the recipient unfollowed meanwhile?

**Answer.** A unique recipient/post key and versioned upsert protect against duplicate materialization; the authoritative per-page release-admission check, after hydration and serialized with changes, decides whether the materialized entry may be released. Worker success does not override viewer policy.

**Q4 (LO2):** With 100k posts/day and average 200 followers, what is the illustrative fan-out write rate, and why is a two-million-follower post special?

**Answer.** 20 million inbox entries/day, about 231/s average. The celebrity creates two million writes from one original post; average rate hides this burst and queue skew. Consider hybrid on-read, explicitly budget hot-author reads.

**Q5 (LO2):** Ben's feed merges pushed Ada items and pulled Zee items. If the same post appears in both paths due to policy migration, should it be returned twice? What else must be checked?

**Answer.** Deduplicate by canonical post ID, apply a stable order and verify viewer eligibility. Cap fan-in and bind each continuation to an immutable candidate view, source cuts, rank version and exclusive last key; a watermark alone cannot freeze membership. A partial source needs an explicit completeness policy.

**Q6 (LO2):** A reply reaches its fan-out destination before its parent. Does worker processing order establish the intended display order? If `a10` reaches the inbox after a first page ending at `a09`, does a watermark plus last key alone guarantee it appears on the next page?

**Answer.** No. Display uses canonical stable ordering and thread parent relationship with visibility checks; queue arrival order or wall-clock processing does not establish causality. The reader may show a thread placeholder or defer the reply by product policy. No: late `a10` sorts ahead of `a09` and can be skipped. Continue the pinned candidate view and expose late arrivals in a new session; expiry requires an explicit restart.

## EX-1 — Two feeds, two posting patterns

At 10:00 Ada (20 followers) commits `a10`. Zee (2 million followers) commits `z55` at 10:00:01. Ben follows both; Cara follows Ada. Fan-out `a10` reaches Cara at 10:00:02, Ben at 10:00:08; two duplicate delivery attempts target Ben. Ben's existing recent inbox contains `a09`. Ben unfollows Ada at 10:00:05 after requesting a second page. His first page at 10:00:03 is the two-item pinned view `z55, a09`, ending at `a09`; `a10` arrives later and is outside that pinned candidate view. A separate reply to `a10` is committed at 10:00:02, but its delivery arrives first. Assume an 800 ms feed deadline and the routine-author ten-second visible freshness target defined in the lesson. In a synthetic five-minute ingress cohort of 1,000 valid original feed opens, the exclusive outcomes are 970 authorized, correct and fresh pages received within 800 ms, 10 stale, 8 late, 7 rejected and 5 failed or unknown. Each original keeps its first-ingress time across retries; the target is at least 99% on-time authorized and fresh. Follow each original to its deadline and record later completion where applicable.

**Task.** Draw the durable write/outbox, worker fan-out, viewer-side merge/hydration and keyword index paths. Compute the illustrative inbox writes/day. Show Ben/Cara's 10:00:03 feed candidates and a reconciled later feed; defend a hybrid threshold policy based on capacity and skew. Show how to resolve duplicate attempts, unfollow, reply ordering and paged reads. Calculate the client-visible original feed SLI and decide against 99%, including the ten-second source freshness rule and all errors. Separate admitted-only latency. Trace both orders of unfollow versus release admission for the second page and a protected media request, and distinguish a pinned candidate set from current authorization. State two experiments that would falsify the proposed design.

### Reference design

1. Post API commits `post_id` and event intent together. User timeline reads the canonical author index; both posts are present after commit. Workers consume stable event IDs and append unique `(recipient_id, post_id)` entries for ordinary Ada; Zee is designated read-time source. Search consumes a separate source-cut projection with possible lag.
2. At 10:00:03, Ben may see pulled `z55` plus existing `a09`, but not the delayed `a10`; Cara sees `a10`. At 10:00:08 after deduplicated delivery, Ben can see `z55, a10, a09`, before applying the new follow policy. Worker retries do not create duplicate `a10` rows.
3. For every page, perform authoritative release admission after fetch and hydration, serialized with Ben’s follow and post-policy changes. If the unfollow commits first, suppress Ada and refill within the pinned candidates, return an allowed short page or fail; if admission wins first, that already admitted response can complete. If authority is unavailable fail closed. A protected media request uses the same admission policy; a previously issued presigned capability remains usable within its scope/lifetime, and already admitted bytes cannot be recalled. The cursor pins viewer, immutable inbox and celebrity-source membership, follow/rank versions, source cut, exclusive last key and expiry. The late `a10` is outside Ben’s 10:00:03 view, so cannot appear on its next page; a new session may include it only if currently authorized. Restart explicitly if the pinned view expires or is lost. A creation watermark alone cannot establish membership.
4. A reply with `parent_post_id=a10` may arrive first via asynchronous delivery, but display respects the thread graph and current parent eligibility. Worker delivery order alone is insufficient.
5. Push traffic ≈20 million entries/day (231/s avg) under the example; Zee's one post is two million writes. A threshold should come from queue completion latency and total fan-out write budget; pulling Zee's posts adds a bounded hot-author read burden.
6. The SLI counts **all 1,000 valid original feed opens in the five-minute ingress cohort** once. Only 970 authorized correct up-to-20 pages, including eligible routine posts committed at least ten seconds before ingress in the page rank range, arrived within 800 ms: **970/1,000 = 97%**, below the 99% target. The 10 stale, 8 late, 7 rejected and 5 failed/unknown originals remain failures. A pinned snapshot must meet the source freshness condition; admitted-only latency is separate. Test a crash after post commit/before enqueue, replayed duplicates, a revocation raced with cache-fill, and an injected slow celebrity read with a strict completeness policy.

### Rubric (12 points)

| Area | Points | Required evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Canonical state and safe dispatch | 3 | Durable post+intent, author timeline, stable IDs and duplicate-safe recipient projection |
| Hybrid feed trace and capacity | 3 | Correct 10:00:03/10:00:08 candidates, 20M/day and celebrity skew reasoning |
| Eligibility and ordering | 3 | Serialized per-page and media release admission with both race orders, fail-closed behavior and capability limits; reply parent handling, dedup and pinned candidate/view continuation including late a10 and expiry |
| Search/ops and user SLO | 3 | Separate index, two falsifying tests and 970/1,000 = 97% vs 99% for all originals, five-minute cohort, client receipt/800 ms and ten-second freshness; exclude no stale, late, rejected or failed requests, and report admitted-only latency separately |

An equivalent pull-only or push-only design can earn full credit if its quantified costs and failure contracts satisfy the scenario.
