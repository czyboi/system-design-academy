# M10-L02 assessment — Social feed publication and timelines

## Six checks

**Q1 (LO1):** A post row commits but a crash occurs before a separate fan-out enqueue. The API already replied “published.” What invariant is missing?

**Answer.** A durable runnable event intent needs to be committed with the post, or a proven recovery scanner must rediscover the committed post. Otherwise a durable author post can remain permanently absent from home timelines. A queue acknowledgment alone does not imply recipients were updated.

**Q2 (LO1):** Why can Ada's user timeline show `a10` before Ben's home timeline does, even though `POST /posts` succeeded?

**Answer.** The author timeline reads the canonical committed post by author. Ben's home inbox is a delayed projection fed by an asynchronous worker; its freshness has a distinct target and error signals.

**Q3 (LO1):** A fan-out worker retries after a timeout and appends the same `post_id` for one recipient again. What must protect the inbox, and what if the recipient unfollowed meanwhile?

**Answer.** A unique recipient/post key and versioned upsert protect against duplicate materialization; a current relationship/visibility check on serving or an equally strong invalidation mechanism prevents showing no-longer-eligible content. Worker success does not override viewer policy.

**Q4 (LO2):** With 100k posts/day and average 200 followers, what is the illustrative fan-out write rate, and why is a two-million-follower post special?

**Answer.** 20 million inbox entries/day, about 231/s average. The celebrity creates two million writes from one original post; average rate hides this burst and queue skew. Consider hybrid on-read, explicitly budget hot-author reads.

**Q5 (LO2):** Ben's feed merges pushed Ada items and pulled Zee items. If the same post appears in both paths due to policy migration, should it be returned twice? What else must be checked?

**Answer.** Deduplicate by canonical post ID, apply a stable order and verify viewer eligibility. Cap fan-in and propagate an upper watermark/cursor so pages do not mix incompatible windows. A partial source needs an explicit completeness policy.

**Q6 (LO2):** A reply reaches its fan-out destination before its parent. Does worker processing order establish the intended display order?

**Answer.** No. Display uses canonical stable ordering and thread parent relationship with visibility checks; queue arrival order or wall-clock processing does not establish causality. The reader may show a thread placeholder or defer the reply by product policy.

## EX-1 — Two feeds, two posting patterns

At 10:00 Ada (20 followers) commits `a10`. Zee (2 million followers) commits `z55` at 10:00:01. Ben follows both; Cara follows Ada. Fan-out `a10` reaches Cara at 10:00:02, Ben at 10:00:08; two duplicate delivery attempts target Ben. Ben's existing recent inbox contains `a09`. A user un-follows Ada at 10:00:05 after requesting a second page. A separate reply to `a10` is committed at 10:00:02, but its delivery arrives first. Assume an 800 ms feed deadline and the routine-author ten-second visible freshness target defined in the lesson.

**Task.** Draw the durable write/outbox, worker fan-out, viewer-side merge/hydration and keyword index paths. Compute the illustrative inbox writes/day. Show Ben/Cara's 10:00:03 feed candidates and a reconciled later feed; defend a hybrid threshold policy based on capacity and skew. Show how to resolve duplicate attempts, unfollow, reply ordering and paged reads. Define a client-visible original feed SLI, including errors and stale exclusions. State two experiments that would falsify the proposed design.

### Reference design

1. Post API commits `post_id` and event intent together. User timeline reads the canonical author index; both posts are present after commit. Workers consume stable event IDs and append unique `(recipient_id, post_id)` entries for ordinary Ada; Zee is designated read-time source. Search consumes a separate source-cut projection with possible lag.
2. At 10:00:03, Ben may see pulled `z55` plus existing `a09`, but not the delayed `a10`; Cara sees `a10`. At 10:00:08 after deduplicated delivery, Ben can see `z55, a10, a09`, before applying the new follow policy. Worker retries do not create duplicate `a10` rows.
3. Ben's unfollow at 10:00:05 must affect subsequent protected reads according to a specified authorization/version boundary, even if his cached inbox still contains `a10`. A cursor from before unfollow needs eligibility re-filtering and stable snapshot/upper-watermark rules; it should not resurrect old posts.
4. A reply with `parent_post_id=a10` may arrive first via asynchronous delivery, but display respects the thread graph and current parent eligibility. Worker delivery order alone is insufficient.
5. Push traffic ≈20 million entries/day (231/s avg) under the example; Zee's one post is two million writes. A threshold should come from queue completion latency and total fan-out write budget; pulling Zee's posts adds a bounded hot-author read burden.
6. The proposed SLI counts **all valid original feed opens in a five-minute ingress cohort**, successful only when client receives a correct authorized up-to-20 page within 800 ms and eligible routine posts older than ten seconds are present. Duplicated retries count once, and failed, late, wrong, stale and rejected originals remain failures. Test a crash after post commit/before enqueue, replayed duplicates, a revocation raced with cache-fill, and an injected slow celebrity read with a strict completeness policy.

### Rubric (12 points)

| Area | Points | Required evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Canonical state and safe dispatch | 3 | Durable post+intent, author timeline, stable IDs and duplicate-safe recipient projection |
| Hybrid feed trace and capacity | 3 | Correct 10:00:03/10:00:08 candidates, 20M/day and celebrity skew reasoning |
| Eligibility and ordering | 3 | Unfollow/revocation policy, reply parent handling, dedup and cursor/window stability |
| Search/ops and user SLO | 3 | Separate index, two falsifying tests and original-operation success denominator |

An equivalent pull-only or push-only design can earn full credit if its quantified costs and failure contracts satisfy the scenario.
