# M10-L03 assessment — Crawler and web-search composition

## Knowledge checks

**Q1 (LO1).** Two URLs on different hosts return byte-identical bodies with hash H. Can the crawler safely discard all metadata for the second URL?

**Answer.** No. It may deduplicate the immutable body after trustworthy collision checks, but each URL retains independent origin, fetch history, links, robots/policy and tombstone state. Search may cluster representatives by a declared policy, not treat the origins as one authoritative URL.

**Q2 (LO1).** A reducer sees URL `u1` twice and `u2` once. Contrast “emit keys with count exactly one” and “emit one copy per distinct key”.

**Answer.** Unique-only filtering emits `u2` but removes `u1` entirely; distinct filtering emits one `u1` and one `u2`. A crawler that wants one fetch candidate per URL needs the latter, with a defined merge rule for priority and source provenance.

**Q3 (LO1).** A fetch of `u1:v2` succeeds, but the index publish event is lost after the page-store commit. What does a successful crawl metric prove and what is required?

**Answer.** It proves only that a page version was persisted. The search index may still serve `v1`. Persist index intent atomically with page-version metadata or recover it deterministically, replay by stable `(url,version)`, update postings/title/snippet and publish a source-cut manifest after required shard acknowledgments.

**Q4 (LO2).** An origin's robots.txt disallows `/private`. Does that enforce secrecy? May an HTTP crawler simply switch to raw UDP to avoid TCP overhead?

**Answer.** No. Robots instructions guide compliant crawlers and aren't authentication; pages need access controls. Arbitrary UDP datagrams do not implement HTTP. HTTP/3 uses negotiated QUIC and HTTP semantics, whereas other versions have their specified transports.

**Q5 (LO2).** Two near-duplicate product pages differ only in a crucial price or legal disclaimer. Is Jaccard similarity above 0.95 sufficient proof they are interchangeable?

**Answer.** No. Similarity is a heuristic dependent on representation and tokenization. A rare legally significant change can leave high overlap. Retain URL/version identity and preserve the exact fields when making search representatives or deduplication decisions.

**Q6 (LO2).** A search response has all shards from generation `g17` but an observed source page version already persisted for `g18`. Is the result complete and fresh?

**Answer.** It may be complete **within g17**, but not fresh relative to the declared source-cut rule. The service should report both coverage and freshness, or fail an export requiring both. Never mix shard generations to claim one complete ranking.

## EX-1 — Crawl a duplicate, process a change, answer a query

**Inputs.** At 10:00, distinct URLs `u1` and `u2` return identical `Blue kettle 2 L` bodies. A 10:10 recrawl of `u1` returns `Blue coffee maker 2 L`; the page is durably stored as `v2`, but its index event times out. Search generation `g17` contains the older version. An origin may return a temporary `503`; a third page links back to `u1`. Crawl quota 2m successful fetches/day at 50KiB average; retention 30 days. Assume 2.5m fetch attempts/day including retries/failures, and a burst of 100 successful 50-KiB bodies/s. Independently assume 10m frontier URL records at 256 bytes each, and 5m unique live indexed documents averaging 200 postings of 12 bytes each; replicas, dictionaries, metadata, compression and other traffic are extra. A strict export requires all shards and a verified source cut no older than five minutes at first ingress. In a synthetic five-minute cohort of 1,000 original valid exports, 970 correct complete fresh responses arrive at the managed client within 800 ms, 10 are incomplete, 8 stale, 7 late and 5 rejected/failed/unknown; target 99%. Separately, an hour cohort has 1,000 original eligible persisted changes: 960 become visible on all required readers within five minutes of their own persistence, 15 have partial reader coverage, 10 are late, 10 failed and 5 unknown; target 99%. Each list is exclusive; all inputs are illustrative.

**Task.** Estimate raw daily/30-day bytes, frontier and posting payload sizes, mean successful fetches and attempts per second, and average/peak payload ingress bandwidth, stating omitted overhead and distinct populations. Define the frontier key/lifecycle and a per-host scheduling constraint. Trace the two URLs through exact deduplication without losing their identities, then the changed `u1:v2` through page-store event and generation publication. Explain a search for `coffee maker` before/after `g18`, and how a temporary error, robots disallow and URL loop affect scheduling. Correct the unique-only filtering bug. Calculate both client-facing export and persisted-change index SLIs against their separate targets, naming each cohort, endpoints, deadline and failures. Explain source-cut age versus response latency, admitted-only latency and not-yet-fetched coverage; add a failure injection.

### Worked solution

- Raw bytes ≈ `2m × 50KiB = 95 GiB/day`, ≈2.8 TiB over 30 days (before compression, duplicates, index and replica overhead). Mean ≈23.15 successful fetches/s; 2.5m attempts/day ≈28.94/s excludes separate robots/DNS requests. The retained-body daily volume corresponds to ≈9.48 Mbit/s average payload ingress; 100 bodies/s at 50 KiB gives 40.96 Mbit/s peak payload ingress, before headers, retries, redirects, encoding, egress and replication. Frontier: 10m × 256 B = 2.56 GB ≈2.38 GiB per copy; postings: 5m unique documents × 200 × 12 B = 12 GB ≈11.18 GiB per copy. These independent populations are synthetic; index/queue metadata, compression and replica costs remain extra.
- Normalize URIs conservatively to a durable `(fetch_key, next_due_at, fetch_generation)` record: exclude fragments from HTTP fetch keys but retain raw discovery provenance; optional hash-route rendering uses a distinct bounded render key. Merge duplicate discoveries into one scheduled record with capped max priority and existing earliest due; leased updates are merged into future recrawl, not parallel fetches, and generation checks preserve updates. Leasing is recoverable. Host-level concurrency and robots policy gate dispatch, and a cycle only updates already-known URL scheduling rather than recursing forever.
- Both `u1` and `u2` retain their own URL identities/provenance. Body H can be stored once if the bytes are verified identical. A distinct reducer emits **one** candidate per URL even when a URL appears multiple times, unlike unique-only filtering.
- `u1:v2` has a durable page version and pending index job. A missing acknowledgment is **unknown**, not proof that the update never committed. Query and/or retry by stable version; only publish `g18` after relay and durable application through the source cut and query-visible confirmation on every eligible required-shard reader, including atomic visibility of the new document's title, snippet and postings and removal of the old terms. A `g17` search for `coffee maker` misses `u1`; `g18` includes it. Strict export against the required cut fails while freshness is unproven.
- Back off on 503, honor RFC9309 rules and failure policy, and never use robots as authorization. Export success is 970/1,000 = 97% against 99%, measured from original ingress to managed-client receipt within 800 ms in a five-minute arrival cohort, requiring a correct complete generation and source cut at most five minutes old at ingress; incomplete, stale, late and rejected/failed/unknown remain failures. The separate persisted-change SLI is 960/1,000 = 96% against 99% for the one-hour persistence cohort, requiring visibility on all eligible readers within five minutes of each durable commit; partial, late, failed and unknown remain failures. The source-cut age is not client response time; measure admitted-only latency and not-yet-fetched origin coverage separately.
- Falsify by crashing after page persistence but before event dispatch, replay `v1` late and check that `g18` does not regress, while measuring wrong/old search results at client.
