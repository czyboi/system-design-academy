# DNS and endpoint discovery assessment

## Practical exercise

EX-1. A reporting service is preparing an A/B rollout across two regions. Two authoritative records are eligible, with weights 2 and 1 for regions East and West. A shared recursive resolver serves 800 clients, each issuing one request. Another resolver serves 100 clients, each issuing one request. Each resolver makes one selection and caches its answer for the full exercise. The DNS service applies the stated weights when selecting answers. East will undergo maintenance after the selection.

Deliver a short decision note that explains the weighted share of answer selections, why it cannot predict the exact request split, and the client effect of a changed authoritative answer. Propose one observation and one mitigation for the maintenance. Assume clients honor DNS cache entries and use a new connection for each request.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

What does a recursive resolver need to obtain when it encounters a delegation it has not cached?

**Answer:** It follows the delegation to locate the child zone's authoritative name servers and queries toward an answer.

**Explanation:** The parent delegation provides NS information for the next authority.

### Q2

A CNAME for api.example points to edge.example. What additional information is needed before an IPv4 client can connect?

**Answer:** The client or resolver needs an IPv4 address for the canonical target.

**Explanation:** The alias gives a canonical name, while an A record supplies an IPv4 address.

### Q3

At time zero an operator changes an authoritative answer. A resolver cached the old record 40 seconds earlier with TTL 120 seconds. Assuming ordinary TTL honoring, no stale service and no reused connection, can a new request at t = 50 s still use the old answer?

**Answer:** Yes. The cached record expires at t = 80 s.

**Explanation:** Its age at the request is 90 seconds, less than the 120-second TTL.

### Q4

Why does reducing the configured TTL immediately before maintenance fail to speed up every cached answer?

**Answer:** Answers already cached under the old TTL retain their remaining cache lifetime.

**Explanation:** The new TTL applies when a resolver obtains the newer record.

### Q5

A service needs an approximate 3-to-1 rollout across two eligible regions. Which DNS option fits, and what outcome must the team avoid assuming?

**Answer:** A 3-to-1 weighted answer policy fits the coarse rollout. It does not assure a 3-to-1 share of individual requests.

**Explanation:** Each resolver may reuse one selected answer for a different number of clients and requests.

### Q6

How does a geolocation policy differ from a latency policy, and what must a team verify?

**Answer:** Geolocation selects by inferred geographic location, while latency selection uses the provider's latency data. Verify the resulting client experience and policy fallback.

**Explanation:** The inferred location and provider measurements may not match a particular client's end-to-end latency.

## Answer key / rubric

A sample decision note expects an answer-selection share of two thirds East and one third West across eligible selections. With only two cached selections, the 800-client resolver could select either region. The actual request split can therefore differ substantially from those shares. After an authoritative change, resolvers continue returning their cached answer until expiry under the exercise assumptions. The team can keep East available while caches drain, and observe requests arriving at both regions. A different mitigation is acceptable when its assumptions and failure effects are explicit.

Score the exercise out of 10 points.

- 2 points for the correct eligible answer-selection proportions.
- 3 points for explaining resolver sharing and why request proportions differ.
- 2 points for tracing cached answers after the change.
- 3 points for a concrete observation and feasible maintenance mitigation.
