# Source evidence for M02-L01

Checked 2026-11-11. Confidence is verified for the scoped claim in each row. Scenario inputs, calculations and design judgments are original illustrative assumptions.

| Claim | Supported statement | Primary evidence and version | Scope and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DNS-1 | On a cache miss a recursive resolver can follow referrals to authoritative servers, follow an alias and cache records for their remaining TTL. | [IETF RFC 1034 §§3.6, 4.3.2 and 5.3.3 (1987)](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc1034#section-5.3.3) | Caching is per record set and resolver. TTL applies to DNS data, separate from connection lifetime. |
| DNS-2 | A, NS, MX, CNAME and TXT record data express IPv4 addresses, zone name servers, mail exchange with preference, canonical names and text. AAAA carries IPv6. | [IETF RFC 1035 §§3.2.2, 3.3, 3.4.1 and RFC 3596 §2 (1987, 2003)](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc1035#section-3.3) | See also [RFC 3596 §2](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc3596#section-2). An MX or NS target is a host name. A CNAME owner cannot also hold ordinary data. |
| DNS-3 | Weighted authoritative answers, latency routing, geography routing and active-passive failover are separate Route 53 policies. A weight gives an answer-selection share proportional to the eligible group. | [AWS Route 53 Developer Guide, Choosing a routing policy and Weighted routing (living documentation)](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/Route53/latest/DeveloperGuide/routing-policy-weighted.html) | See also [Choosing a routing policy](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/Route53/latest/DeveloperGuide/routing-policy.html). Resolver fan-out, TTL, client behavior and health policy prevent exact per-request quotas. |
| DNS-4 | Resolvers may use expired cached data when refresh fails under a configured serve-stale policy. | [IETF RFC 8767 §§4 and 5 (2020)](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8767#section-4) | Conditional extension. Do not use TTL as a universal deadline for migration. |
