# M12-L04 assessment — Call centre lifecycle

## Knowledge checks

**Q1 (LO1).** All employees are busy. C10 arrives and then O1 becomes idle. What state transitions must happen together?

**Answer.** C10 leaves its single waiting queue membership and gets one versioned assignment to idle O1, while O1 becomes busy with C10. Partial queue removal without assignment or assignment without removing the queue entry is unsafe.

**Q2 (LO1).** O1 escalates C10 to supervisor, and S1 picks up assignment v2. O1's old completion for v1 arrives. What happens?

**Answer.** Stale v1 must not complete C10 or clear the current S1 assignment. The guard compares call and active assignment IDs/versions; return stale/conflict or the old command's receipt.

**Q3 (LO1).** Can an operator handle a director-ranked call? May a director handle an operator-ranked call under the chosen policy?

**Answer.** No for the operator: rank too low. Yes for director under the lesson's immediate-eligible-higher-rank fallback; the reserved-specialist alternative is a different explicit policy.

**Q4 (LO2).** A `complete` event arrives twice after a different call is assigned to the same employee. Why is `employee.status=idle` unsafe?

**Answer.** The employee has a new active assignment. Completion must be conditional on the old matching assignment and idempotent receipt; unconditional freeing creates overassignment.

**Q5 (LO2).** What is a design reason to prefer rank policy composition to three Employee subclasses?

**Answer.** Rank eligibility and queue selection are changeable policy, while assignment lifecycle is shared. Separate DispatchPolicy and Assignment avoids each subclass mutating global state and simplifies changing fallback/queue rules.

**Q6 (LO2).** How do no-VIP queues still starve calls?

**Answer.** An endless flow of eligible work plus an unspecified scheduling priority can keep lower-priority queues waiting. Explicit fair queue choice or bounded waiting is required independently of VIP classification.

## EX-1 — Dispatch and stale completion

**Scenario.** One operator O1, supervisor S1 and director D1 process C9 and C10. Ranks are ordered and the policy assigns the lowest available eligible employee (higher ranks can handle lower-ranked calls). Initially O1/S1 busy, D1 idle. C9 arrives; D1 takes C9. C10 arrives and waits. O1 becomes free, receives C10 v1, escalates it, then S1 takes C10 v2. Duplicated v1 completion races with S1's v2 completion, while a new C11 waits for an operator.

**Task.** Draw Call, Assignment, Employee, Dispatcher and DispatchPolicy responsibilities; tabulate states/owners, guards and queue effects; handle stale complete, eligible fallback and employee reuse; compare specialist reservation; specify 5 invariants and test under concurrency. Define original-call SLO denominator.

### Reference solution

C9 is assigned to D1 by the declared fallback policy. C10 waits once until O1 is free, then atomically moves from queue to v1 assignment. Escalation retires v1, raises min_rank to supervisor, and assigns S1 only after the old owner is cleared. The stale v1 completion cannot change C10 at v2 or free O1's later C11 assignment. Completing v2 clears S1 and closes C10 once. Invariants: at most one active assignment per call; at most one active call per employee; queued and assigned disjoint; only eligible ranks; completed state terminal. Coarse single-owner lock or coherent state transition is acceptable; queue worker races cannot bypass version guard. Per-original timely connection metric uses all arrivals, including shed/timeouts/late, not only accepted calls.
