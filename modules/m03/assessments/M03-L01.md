# Companion assessment — Application layers and microservices

## Practical exercise

EX-1. A ticketing application sees 600 reservation requests per minute at peak. A reservation of up to four seats must atomically claim all requested seats and record the reservation. The interactive pool has 8 CPU cores; PDF generation saturates it during bursts. Receipts may appear up to one minute later. A proposal creates separate synchronous services for seats, reservations and customers, plus a PDF service. A worker can finish a PDF and lose its reply before the reservation owner records completion.

Trace the web, application and data responsibilities. Name the owner of the invariant and explain concurrent claims, durable work initiation and retries across boundaries. Compare the proposal with an alternative, trace the lost reply and user-visible partial state, and specify production observations and a failure test that would support your choice.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

Which layer applies a rule that a catalogue item may only be published after an ownership check?

Answer: The application layer applies and coordinates the business rule.

Explanation: The web layer handles the request boundary, and the data layer persists or retrieves state used by the rule.

### Q2

Can the web, application and data responsibilities appear in a single deployable application?

Answer: Yes.

Explanation: A logical layer describes responsibility, while a deployment tier describes where components run and communicate.

### Q3

In the media example, which owner validates and records the conversion result before publication state changes?

Answer: The catalogue owner.

Explanation: The processor performs conversion, while the catalogue retains the publication decision and its state.

### Q4

Why might an entity-per-service design increase the failure surface for one metadata request?

Answer: A request requiring several synchronous service calls depends on each called service and network path.

Explanation: A stalled or failed required dependency can delay or fail the request.

### Q5

When is a separate media processor a defensible choice in the worked example?

Answer: When conversion CPU is the measured constraint and delayed completion is acceptable.

Explanation: The processor can receive its own capacity allocation, while the design must handle the additional coordination and intermediate state.

### Q6

Why does a saga need an explicit recovery plan for a failed compensation?

Answer: A compensation is another business operation and can itself fail.

Explanation: Cross-service local transactions do not provide one automatic atomic rollback of the whole workflow.

## Answer key / rubric

One viable answer has the web boundary accept the reservation, the application validate the request, and the reservation owner conditionally claim each still-available seat in one transaction. If any claim fails, roll back every claim and report a conflict. Otherwise commit the claims, reservation and pending receipt work together before acknowledging. A separately deployed PDF processor is allocated CPU independently. The response includes `receipt pending`. A stable work ID, durable processor deduplication and an idempotent result transition at the reservation owner make redelivery safe within their stated retention contracts; a lost reply leaves the receipt pending until the result is recovered. If the processor is unavailable, the reservation remains committed and pending work is retried. The entity-per-service proposal adds synchronous dependencies and would need a cross-service invariant and recovery design; independent seat and reservation commits can leave a partial reservation. Measure reservation latency, CPU saturation, pending age and duplicate effects under the stated peak; inject a lost worker reply and restart to verify eventual recovery and one recorded result. A modular monolith can instead reserve CPU for the interactive pool and cap receipt-worker CPU; compare measured latency and pending age against the one-minute requirement.

Score 10 points: 1 for the layer trace; 3 for naming the invariant owner and a concurrency-safe all-or-nothing claim; 2 for durable work and duplicate-safe cross-boundary recovery; 2 for a justified resource boundary, alternative and partial-failure consequences; 2 for concrete production signals and a failure test. For each criterion, full credit requires its mechanism and consequence or verification, while a plausible but unqualified choice earns at most half of that criterion. Accept justified alternatives that preserve the invariant and recovery contract.
