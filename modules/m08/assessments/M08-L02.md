# Companion assessment for M08-L02

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** A dashboard counts distinct product requests by event ID for the event-time hour `[12:00, 13:00)`. IDs are globally unique and retries use the same ID and payload. Per hour, serialized durable intake assigns an arrival sequence, first durable intake time and live-or-correction classification. Admissions strictly before 13:05 are live-eligible. The durable close barrier is serialized at 13:05, and admissions at or after it go to corrections. The live processor drains through the barrier before final publication. Publish after each accepted event and at 13:00. An independently retained authoritative archive offers complete, immutable, nested cuts containing all live admissions through the barrier and corrections up to the cut. Break ties by product ID ascending.

| First durable intake time | Event `(ID, event time, product)` |
| --- | --- |
| 12:10 | `(a, 12:09, ink)` |
| 12:20 | `(b, 12:19, paper)` |
| 12:50 | `(c, 12:49, ink)` |
| 13:01 | retry of `a` |
| 13:04 | `(d, 12:58, paper)` |
| 13:05 | `(e, 12:45, paper)` |

1. Give the counts and leader at 13:00, after d, and at finalization. The processor crashes after d's durable intake but before applying it, then recovers after 13:05. Explain how the final live result remains correct and when final publication may occur. Explain what happens to e and calculate a corrected result if a complete archive cut contains all five unique IDs.
2. A later cut additionally contains a sixth unique paper event. Give a replay and sink contract, binding revisions to cuts, and trace the newer repair publishing before the older one. Separately, trace correction A publishing before the stalled live processor drains, followed by its final intent and an older provisional retry. State when live state can retire, how same-cut retries behave and the retention cost of allowing more live lateness.
3. In a different complete window, feed the two-counter Space-Saving summary the unit events `A, B, A, C`. Show its final counter estimates and error bounds. Explain whether it proves a strictly greatest frequency and whether exact counts are required to resolve that distinction.
4. Current counts and comparable hourly baselines are ink 60 versus 30 and paper 20 versus 2. Rank both by count and by growth ratio. Explain how a minimum-volume rule changes the decision.
5. Choose Storm Core 2.8.1 or Spark Structured Streaming 4.0.1 for this service and describe the required source, processing and sink recovery conditions. Another defensible choice earns full credit if those conditions are addressed.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

Why does the lesson's event e4 change the 10:00 hour after 11:00?

**Answer.** Its event time is 10:54, so it belongs to `[10:00, 11:00)`. Its durable intake before 11:05 makes it live-eligible, and processing it publishes a revised provisional result.

### Q2

The lesson's retry of e1 arrives with the same ID and payload at 11:02. What happens?

**Answer.** The retained seen-ID state identifies a duplicate, so the count stays unchanged. This is a business-ID rule even if the retry has a different source offset.

### Q3

What does a heuristic watermark establish about an event timestamp earlier than the watermark?

**Answer.** It is an estimate of event-time progress and does not prove that no earlier event can arrive. The processing system's window and cleanup policy determines the effect of a later arrival. A perfect watermark with sufficient source knowledge can have a stronger guarantee.

### Q4

Why can a two-counter Space-Saving result leave a tie uncertain?

**Answer.** A replacement inherits the old minimum in its estimate and records that amount as possible error. Overlapping frequency bounds can leave frequency equality unresolved. Exact counts can settle a decision that requires that distinction, though a specified ID tie-break may already decide the displayed rank.

### Q5

Why might a topic with the most lifetime requests fail to be the most trending topic this hour?

**Answer.** Lifetime total measures accumulated popularity. A trend policy compares the current window with its chosen baseline, so a smaller topic with a larger relative increase can rank higher.

### Q6

Does a Storm acknowledgement or Spark checkpoint alone prevent duplicate external effects?

**Answer.** Neither alone establishes the whole external effect contract. Storm needs a reliable re-emitting spout and appropriately anchored acknowledgements, and a retry-safe sink. Spark's replay guarantee depends on compatible sources and sinks. A separate event-ID rule handles duplicate business events.

## Reference solution and rubric

At 13:00 ink has 2 and paper has 1, so ink leads. The retry of a is ignored. The 13:04 durable admission of d records it as live-eligible. After the crash, recovery reuses that classification, applies d once and drains through the close barrier. Paper becomes 2, producing a tie with ink 2, and alphabetical tie-breaking keeps ink first. The final live result is the same and publishes only after the drain, which can be later than 13:05. Event e's intake is at the cutoff, so it goes to corrections. A complete archived replay deduplicated by ID gives ink 2 and paper 3, so paper leads in a corrected revision.

Commit progress, seen IDs, counts and output intent atomically, or use an equivalent recoverable protocol. Retry output intents idempotently. A repair reads a complete immutable archive cut including the close barrier, resolves ID conflicts and produces a full replacement. Before computation a durable coordinator binds a stable revision to each cut in increasing nested-cut order. For cut A through e, revision 1 yields ink 2 and paper 3. Later cut B includes the sixth paper event and receives revision 2, yielding ink 2 and paper 4. If B publishes first, the sink accepts revision 2 and rejects A's later revision 1. Same-cut retries reuse the revision and payload with no additional effect. The sink rejects conflicting same-revision payloads.

The sink enforces provisional writes ordered by live sequence, followed by final live, followed by increasing coverage-bound correction revisions. A provisional retry loses after final or correction, and final loses after correction. In the separate correction-first trace, A commits ink 2, paper 3 while live processing is stalled. After recovery, the processor drains d and the close barrier and persists its final intent for ink 2, paper 2. The sink suppresses that final and a delayed provisional write. The processor can retire live counts and seen IDs after authoritative confirmation of A, because A's complete cut covers the live final state. In the ordinary case it can retire after confirmation of its matching final write. Both paths require drain and durable terminal intent first. A larger live acceptance horizon retains both per-key counts and seen IDs longer. Business-ID deduplication handles repeated events under different offsets.

The summary ends with `(A, 2, 0)` and `(C, 2, 1)`. A's true count is 2, and C's is between 1 and 2. The estimates do not establish whether C ties A, so they do not prove a strictly greatest frequency. With ascending ID tie-breaking A still ranks first. Exact replay gives A 2, B 1 and C 1. Ink leads by count, 60 versus 20. Paper leads by growth, 10 times baseline versus ink's 2 times. A minimum-current-volume threshold of 10 admits both, while one above 20 excludes paper. A zero baseline needs an explicit separate policy.

For Spark, one defensible design uses a replayable source, checkpointed micro-batches and a compatible sink that supports the required replacement semantics. Deduplicate stable business IDs for the defined live horizon and use the archived repair path beyond it. For Storm Core, use a reliable spout capable of re-emitting, correctly anchor and acknowledge tuple trees, and make the sink retry-safe. Both choices require the stated correction and revision contract.
