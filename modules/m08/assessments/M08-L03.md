# Assessment — indexes and scatter-gather search

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

The index analyzer lowercases and splits on spaces. What postings are consulted for `BLUE mug` under AND, and how are candidates selected?

**Answer.** Look up `blue` and `mug`, then intersect their ID lists. Compatible query analysis is required for the same terms to be addressed.

### Q2

In the lesson's trace, what are A's two ranked candidates and their scores?

**Answer.** `d2:3` then `d1:2`. Each occurrence of either term scores one point, and both documents match both terms.

### Q3

What happens to a deletion at revision 6 when a delayed full replacement at revision 5 is replayed into a generation that retains deletion markers?

**Answer.** The conditional apply sees the revision 6 marker and ignores revision 5. Without the marker the stale full value could reintroduce the document.

### Q4

Why can a coordinator request only the local top two from each shard in the lesson's full-result example?

**Answer.** Each shard owns disjoint documents and applies the same eligibility and total order. Any omitted candidate already has two documents ahead of it on that shard. This conclusion requires reconsideration if a later reranker or deduplication changes that order.

### Q5

One of three shards times out. Why can the response be both partial and fresh?

**Answer.** The responding shards may already reflect recent source changes, but the missing shard can contain a higher-ranked match. Freshness and completeness are separate properties. Label the answer incomplete or fail according to the chosen contract.

### Q6

A rebuild's required log continuation is no longer retained. Should the indexer move its cursor to the earliest available position?

**Answer.** No. Missing records could contain updates or deletes. Protect a new earlier end vector, take a consistent source snapshot with deletion markers, and replay with version-aware application. Fence old writers and make effects through the captured barrier visible on every eligible search reader before the verified cutover.

## EX-1 — choose a search contract

Use the lesson's document shards, analyzer, AND query and score. A and B respond before the shard deadline, and C times out. A user-facing operation succeeds under the service's complete-response target only if every required shard contributes and the complete answer reaches the client within 200 ms of the original valid search's arrival. The measurement window is one hour. Count each original valid search once. The target is 99%.

During one assumed hour, 1,000 original valid searches arrive. Of those, 960 receive complete responses within 200 ms, 15 receive labelled partial responses within 200 ms, 10 receive complete responses after 200 ms, 10 time out or fail, and 5 are rejected. These categories are exclusive.

1. Calculate the partial top two and explain what the missing shard can change. Give the full top two if C later returns its lesson data.
2. Calculate complete on-time success and decide whether the target is met. Name two additional metrics that diagnose the result.
3. Propose a response policy for a catalogue browsing screen and one for a workflow that requires the correct complete global top two. Give one cost or failure mode of hedging.
4. A source deletion was applied to the old generation and already became visible to search while a replacement index is being built. Describe the snapshot, replay and cutover conditions needed to keep the deleted item from reappearing. Explain the different case in which another committed deletion is still waiting in the outbox at the captured log barrier.

### Sample answer

1. A and B yield `d2:3, d3:3`, ordered by ID for the tie. Mark the result incomplete. C can contain a higher score. With C's `d5:4`, the full top two are `d5:4, d2:3`.
2. The numerator is 960 and denominator 1,000, so success is 96%, below 99%. Partial, late, failed, timed-out and rejected searches all miss this complete-response target. Record partial-result rate and successful-response latency separately, and inspect missing-shard rate or visibility lag to diagnose cause.
3. Browsing can return an explicitly labelled partial answer when the product accepts missing matches. A workflow requiring the correct complete global top two should fail the request at the deadline and allow a later retry if a required shard is unavailable. A hedge can increase load on shared resources and worsen the delay. A hedge must target a compatible replica and contribute at most one response for its logical shard.
4. Protect the per-partition end vector before the consistent snapshot and retain it through replay. Include source revision and durable deletion markers, then replay complete replacements with a conditional newest-revision check. Fence and settle old writers and capture the exclusive post-fence barrier. Process its required prefix durably, make it visible and verify the actual search path on every eligible reader of every required shard before switching reads atomically. Pin each request and fetch to one generation and expected shard set. If continuation is lost, start a new protected rebuild. A deletion still waiting in the outbox may be outside the barrier and appear until normal processing catches up. For a stronger guarantee through a chosen source cut, prove relay publication through that cut before capturing the barrier, then apply and expose it before cutover.
