# Reference design and rubric

## Aggregate once per logical input, then sort

Job 1 maps each sale to `(category, quantity)`. Use one logical contribution per record in the input snapshot. For illustration send Tea and Filters to reducer X and Mugs to Y. A replacement attempt for split A writes attempt-scoped intermediate output. The coordinator's accepted output for A supplies all reducer buckets for that logical split, without consuming both attempts' outputs as separate contributions. A reducer publishes only one completed result per logical output partition. This handles execution retry while the input snapshot or a deduplication step handles duplicate source events. [M08-L01](../lessons/M08-L01.md).

| Reducer | Grouped values | Totals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X | `Tea [6, 1, 2]`, `Filters [4]` | `Tea 9`, `Filters 4` |
| Y | `Mugs [3, 5]` | `Mugs 8` |

Job 2 emits ordered keys `(-9, Tea)`, `(-4, Filters)` and `(-8, Mugs)` from the completed totals. One reducer sorts this small result and emits `[Tea 9, Mugs 8, Filters 4]`. At larger category counts, range-partition the same composite key and read partition files in range order. Sorting within independently hashed partitions would leave an unordered set of files. If Tea dominates shuffle bytes and one reducer's execution time, combine local sums and inspect the resulting per-reducer bytes and task duration. If the imbalance remains, divide the hot key into stable salt buckets, sum each, and reduce the bucket sums back to one Tea total before ranking. Extra shuffle and recovery work need a measured benefit.

## Keep provisional totals revisable

Event time places `s1`–`s8` in `[10:00, 11:00)`; the serialized **durable first-admission order**, not the progress estimate, determines which distinct IDs may enter live state. Initialize exact sums and deduplication IDs from the immutable prefix and persist them with its exclusive continuation `p6` and initial publication intent as one recoverable state. Suppose `s4` is redelivered after snapshot capture but before the live reader starts: it is retained at or after `p6`, so the reader sees it and its stored ID suppresses the duplicate. An unseen distinct admission at that position would instead be counted; looking up a later end could skip it. The ranking published by 11:01 is `[Tea 9, Mugs 8, Filters 4]` (provisional). The duplicate `s4`, admitted at 11:01:30 with the same payload, leaves it unchanged. First admission of `s7` at 11:02 raises Mugs by 3 and publishes the newer *provisional* whole result `[Mugs 11, Tea 9, Filters 4]`. An input retry must reuse its recorded identity, sequence and classification; it cannot increment a live or archive total twice.

The durable **11:05 close marker follows `s7`**. Its event-time progress trigger is not evidence of a complete source. Drain every intake sequence before that marker, then persist the terminal intent, progress, deduplication state and counts atomically or through a recoverable equivalent. The sink publishes the same `[Mugs 11, Tea 9, Filters 4]` as a **final live** whole result and durably acknowledges it. A delayed provisional intent cannot overwrite this final. This exercise requires the sink’s durable final receipt **before** permitting any correction write; the live writer need not have received the response yet. A failure to commit final delays correction rather than reclassifying `s7` or bypassing the phase order. The sink atomically persists a terminal receipt keyed by the stable final intent with the final value. If its response is lost, a separate correction publisher may observe that receipt and publish `s8` while the live writer is down. After restart the live writer queries the durable receipt even though the serving value is now corrected; a retry with the same intent and payload returns the same proof without replacing the correction. Delayed provisional or final attempts cannot overwrite a later phase; a conflicting same-intent payload is rejected. Keep the receipt through correction replacement until all live retry and recovery obligations finish. Retire live state only after the terminal intent is durable and the matching receipt is confirmed. The broader [M08-L02](../lessons/M08-L02.md) allows a distinct, properly fenced policy where a complete correction wins before a delayed final, but that is not the selected contract here.

The new `s8` is first admitted at 11:07 and belongs to **archive-only correction** despite its 10:57 event time. For a complete immutable archive cut covering the closed live prefix through `s8`, deduplicate all event IDs, then compute `[Mugs 11, Tea 9, Filters 6]`. Durably assign that cut a stable higher correction revision before computing and publish a full replacement **after** final acknowledgement. The sink accepts only increasing verified-coverage revisions; an older repair finishing later loses, and retrying the same cut with the same revision/payload has no effect. A conflicting payload is rejected. An archive admission at or after 12:00 is escalated instead of silently counted, and all accepted immutable cuts remain recoverable until reconciliation and retry obligations finish. Record source cut, phase and serving revision so readers can distinguish a corrected result from final live.

Use scheduled batch for reproducible reconciliation plus an incremental provisional view if repeated scans cannot satisfy the observed freshness deadline. Exact totals suit the small category dimension. Approximate top-k is defensible only with a product contract that communicates uncertainty, especially for close candidates. [M08-L02](../lessons/M08-L02.md).

## Preserve the difference between fast and complete

Each shard uses its term-to-document postings and intersects the postings for `steel` and `filter`. The supplied matches support a conceptual sketch of that intersection; they do not enumerate the individual term postings. The coordinator sends the query and generation `g17` to all three shards and requests local top two. Under one score order and disjoint document IDs, a candidate dropped below its shard's top two already has at least two global candidates ahead of it. This means the merged head contains the complete global top two if all shards successfully reply from `g17`.

Before issuing a freshness manifest, prove that every relevant committed catalogue change through a chosen source cut was relayed to the index log, apply through its bound log boundary durably, and verify query visibility through that boundary on every eligible reader for the required logical shard set. Bind the proof to `g17`, its shard set and compatible read view; `g17` is immutable for this request in this scenario. Query, fetch, retries and hedges retain the binding and the coordinator accepts at most one reply per logical shard. An indexer checkpoint alone is insufficient if the relay has not emitted a deletion or a reader has not refreshed. These are application proof obligations, not an automatic manifest provided by the index. [M08-L03](../lessons/M08-L03.md).

The full order starts `[c31, a11]`; C's reply at 104 ms misses the 80 ms shard cutoff. Browsing can return `[a11, b21]`, marked `complete=false`, `responded_shards=2/3`, `missing=[C]`, `generation=g17` and a verified freshness cut. It cannot say that `a11` is best across all documents. Export returns an explicit incomplete-search failure, not a short or empty list under a complete label. Late replies must not mutate the answer. A retry within the same original request must retain the 100 ms client deadline and combine only compatible results of the same generation; a replica hedge is useful only when spare capacity and the remaining deadline allow it. Charge the extra attempt against the request's load budget. [M08-L03](../lessons/M08-L03.md).

Only 968 of 1,000 original requests qualified, so user-visible success was **96.8%**, below 98%. Record per-shard reachability and timeout histograms, fan-out deadline and coverage, plus the served generation and verified source cut. Compare client-received status and time against those observations. Even if all three shards reply on time, export must fail if the source cut is older than three minutes or its proof is missing; completeness does not establish freshness. Browse may disclose the actual complete coverage with freshness marked stale or unknown. A full reply from an older generation can still fail freshness. Separate successful-response latency from the whole cohort's success ratio.

Use equivalent algorithms or placement if they preserve the stated boundary and workload. A partial export, unqualified global result, duplicated input or exclusion of failed originals earns no credit for the affected criterion.
