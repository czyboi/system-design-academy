# Source evidence for M08-L03

Checked 2026-11-15. The catalogue, query records, score formula and deadlines are scenario assumptions. Use shared fixed weights and a common tie-breaker to make the three-shard trace reproducible.

| Claim | Primary evidence and scope | Verification and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1. Full-text analysis turns text into tokens. Index-time and query-time analysis must be compatible with matching intent. Boolean filters and scoring clauses have distinct roles. | Elastic [Elasticsearch 8.19 Text Analysis](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/analysis.html) and [Boolean Query](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/query-dsl-bool-query.html). | Verified for the documented engine. Forward documents versus term-to-posting lists and the toy analyzer are teaching models. Eligibility and the example score are defined explicitly. |
| C2. With disjoint document shards, a shared total order and identical match rules, each shard's top k suffices for global top k. Local term statistics can change the meaning of cross-shard scores. | Elastic [Elasticsearch 8.19 Search API, search_type and dfs_query_then_fetch](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/search-search.html#search-search-api-query-params) and [Similarity Settings](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/index-modules-similarity.html). | Verified product distinction between local and distributed term statistics. The top-k rule is a mathematical inference: any omitted local candidate has at least k preceding documents on its own shard under the same total order. Deduplication, global reranking or term-sharded physical plans change the preconditions. |
| C3. A scatter-gather request waiting for all shards inherits their latency tail. It can cut off slow shards or bound hedges, with an explicit result contract. | Dean and Barroso, CACM 2013 [The Tail at Scale, Why Variability Exists? and Within-Request Short-Term Adaptations](https://barroso.org/publications/TheTailAtScale.pdf), and Elastic [Elasticsearch 8.19 Search API, allow_partial_search_results](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/search-search.html#search-search-api-query-params). | Verified mechanism and optional partial behavior. Per-shard independence is an illustration assumption only. A partial result ranks responding shards and does not prove global absence, ranking or freshness. Cancellation and shared-resource overload require explicit treatment. |
| C4. Indexing and query visibility are separate moments. A rebuild can use a fresh generation, complete source snapshot, retained replay overlap, version-aware changes and a verified cutover. | Elastic [Elasticsearch 8.19 Near real-time search](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/near-real-time.html), AWS [Transactional outbox, issues and considerations](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/cloud-design-patterns/transactional-outbox.html#issues-and-considerations), and [M07-L03 source evidence](../../m07/evidence/M07-L03.md), C3 and C7–C8. | Verified refresh behavior for pinned Elastic docs. Rebuild/repair is an application contract composed with M07-L03; account for deletes, retained offsets, revision ordering and readiness on every eligible search path before switching. A post-fence log barrier covers previously served effects under the scenario assumptions, while a committed but unpublished outbox deletion can fall outside it. A stronger source cut requires independently proving relay publication through that cut before capture. |
