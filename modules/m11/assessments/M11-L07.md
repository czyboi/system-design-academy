# M11-L07 assessment — Authoritative games and order books

## Knowledge checks

**Q1 (LO1).** A game server committed Alice's A42 play at sequence 18 but the HTTP response vanished. At state 18 Alice retries identical A42. Should the server validate current turn and reject her as an invalid actor?

**Answer.** It should first identify the existing input-bound A42 receipt and replay its prior result, without executing the move again. Only a new action runs current-turn checks. Reusing the same A42 with different input is an identity conflict.

**Q2 (LO1).** S1 rests for 5 units at ask 101. Buy B1 takes 4 units at limit 102, and only afterward the server processes cancel S1. What are fill and cancel quantities?

**Answer.** B1 trades 4 against S1 at resting price 101 under the lesson's simplified rule. The cancel removes the remaining 1. A cancellation cannot undo the preceding committed fill; the final S1 status must report executed 4, canceled 1.

**Q3 (LO1).** Repeat Q2 with the cancel command serialized before B1. What changes? Does the client timestamp prove which command should win?

**Answer.** S1's remaining 5 is canceled before matching. B1 cannot trade against it and may rest or expire according to its TIF. Client timestamps do not assign canonical order: the authoritative accepted-command sequence does.

**Q4 (LO2).** A system elects a new instrument matcher while the previous one continues writing to a queue. Is election enough to guarantee one execution history?

**Answer.** No. The durable matching-write boundary must reject stale epochs or enforce another exclusive committed-history protocol; otherwise old and new owners can each commit fills.

**Q5 (LO2).** Can instruments A and B always be processed on independent state machines just because their book entries are separate?

**Answer.** Only for the instrument-local matching invariant. Shared account buying power, exposure limits and cross-instrument atomic obligations require additional authoritative coordination. Instrument partitioning by itself is not global risk consistency.

**Q6 (LO2).** A resumed game has a snapshot at sequence 100 and a committed log through 105. What must a recovery do before accepting move 106, and why can a client projection at 102 not certify a lost move?

**Answer.** Load a snapshot tied to a known committed prefix, deterministically replay 101–105 and fence the old owner. The projection at 102 is delayed and does not prove that later command events are absent; consult stable receipts/authoritative committed sequence.

## EX-1 — Two authority boundaries under failure

**Scenario.** Design a turn-based card service with two-player private hands and duplicate/reordered play messages, plus a single-instrument continuous limit-book with fully displayed price/time priority. One match's player reconnects with last_seen_seq=16 after a lost response for a play committed at 18. Separately the exchange starts with S1 sell 5 at 101 ticks, then receives B1 buy 4 at limit 102 and C1 cancel S1. External ingress is concurrent, so either canonical order is possible. During failover an old owner resumes and tries to append a command.

**Constraints.** Authenticated identities, stable request IDs, hidden-card privacy, integer price ticks, deterministic replay, durable receipts, no invented cross-instrument guarantees; clients need definitive outcomes or an explicit unknown/reconciliation state. Supply illustrative five-minute original-operation SLO populations including failures, shedding and lateness.

**Deliverables.** (1) Command schemas, authority and durable event/receipt model; (2) game action retry/reconnect trace; (3) *both* order/cancel serialization outcomes, with fill and cancel units; (4) safe restart and fencing trace; (5) trade-offs between per-game and per-instrument owners and at least four falsifying tests.

### Reference solution

- **Game:** route by game ID to one epoch-fenced reducer, validate member/turn/card against authoritative state, persist sequence+event+canonical-input receipt. A42's lost response is reconciled from its receipt; client with seq 16 receives only authorized snapshot/delta and does not receive other hands. A conflicting payload under A42 fails. Process new stale/illegal actions without mutating state.
- **Book:** route by instrument to an epoch-fenced reducer. Keep integer ticks, best-price then earliest accepted resting order among the specified priority class, remaining units and input-bound receipts. If B1 precedes C1, execute 4 at 101, cancel 1. If C1 precedes B1, cancel 5; B1 cannot fill S1. Do not report the first execution as reversible by cancel.
- **Recovery:** append only under current durable epoch; resume on the committed prefix, replay deterministic events from a compatible snapshot, verify state hash; stop matching or moves if exclusive authority is unproven. Return unknown/reconcile for reply loss.
- **Policy:** instrument sharding does not certify shared-wallet/risk invariants. A successful client action SLI counts original requests and measures definitive results before deadlines; capacity rejects, timeouts and late decisions stay in denominator. Separate book execution correctness and timely admission/status reporting.
- **Tests:** identical retry after lost response; conflicting ID reuse; reconnect from stale sequence without leaking private hands; both order/cancel races; old writer after new epoch; snapshot behind committed log; hot-instrument overload; replay comparing fill ledger and state hash.
