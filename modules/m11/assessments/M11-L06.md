# M11-L06 companion — Distributed API rate limiter

## Knowledge checks

**Q1 (LO1).** A fixed-window quota allows 3 requests per six-second bucket. Three arrive at 5.80/5.90/5.99, and three more at 6.00/6.10/6.20. How many can a fixed limiter admit? What does the literal rolling-six-second cap require?

**Answer.** Fixed may admit all six, three on either side of the bucket boundary. Exact sliding window accepts the first three but rejects the next three while those accepted events remain within the previous six seconds. Fixed-window adherence does not prove rolling-window adherence.

**Q2 (LO1).** A token bucket starts with 3 tokens, refills 1 token per two seconds. Requests arrive at 0,0.1,0.2,0.3,2.0 with cost 1. Which are admitted?

**Answer.** The first three consume the initial tokens (with small partial refills), r4 has fewer than one token, and by t=2 the bucket has refilled enough to admit r5. The exact fraction can depend on stated time precision, but the rule is atomic refill-with-cap then conditional consume.

**Q3 (LO1).** A leaky *shaper* has 3 queue slots and one departure at 2,4,6,... seconds. r1/r2/r3 arrive before 0.3 and are queued. Is r3 “completed” at admission? What happens to r4 at 0.3?

**Answer.** r3 is only queued and is scheduled for t=6, potentially beyond a client deadline. r4 rejects because all three waiting slots are occupied. Leaky shaping smooths departures; a policing variant can reject instead of queue and has different state.

**Q4 (LO2).** G1 and G2 each allow three requests for one tenant in the same interval. Can a sticky gateway guarantee a global limit of three?

**Answer.** No. Two gateways can admit six, and retries, failover and multiple devices break local affinity. An exact global decision needs one serializing authority, safe disjoint quota grants or an explicitly weaker approximate contract.

**Q5 (LO2).** G1 leases 40 of 100 tokens but crashes after using 10. Can its remaining 30 be given immediately to G2, and why?

**Answer.** Not without authoritative proof or fencing that G1 cannot still admit with the old grant. Reusing uncertain quota can cause more than 100 global admissions. A simpler exact scheme strands the 30 until a safe expiry/epoch transition or permanently consumes them for that window, sacrificing available capacity.

**Q6 (LO2).** Quota store is unavailable. Contrast fail-open and fail-closed, and explain whether a successful limiter check ensures a durable business command exists.

**Answer.** Fail-closed rejects and protects exact safety but reduces availability. Fail-open may overshoot the global cap unless it uses provably disjoint preleased capacity. Limiter allow alone does not create business work: a cross-store crash can consume quota without runnable intent; explicit transactional acceptance/recovery is required.

## EX-1 — Global quota during a burst and store loss

**Scenario.** A tenant requests an expensive report API with a global budget of **3 admitted new originals in any rolling six seconds**. r1–r5 arrive at times 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 2.0. The student also sees a separate boundary burst 5.80, 5.90, 5.99, 6.00, 6.10, 6.20. Two independent gateways route requests; each currently uses its own local counter. An authoritative rate-limit store later becomes unavailable after G1 leased a disjoint batch of quota units. A request is durably accepted but its HTTP reply is lost before the client retries.

**Task.** Tabulate fixed, exact sliding, token and leaky queue behavior for the original five requests under the lesson's stated initial conditions. Show a six-admission fixed-window boundary violation and a two-gateway violation. Propose an exact global limiter with consistent key/time/atomic receipt and business intent, and one lower-latency approximate or prelease alternative. State failures under store loss and old holder resurrection, original-operation acceptance SLI versus quota-safety metric, and at least three falsifying tests.

### Reference solution

- First burst: fixed window admits r1–r3 and rejects r4/r5; exact sliding does the same. Token bucket capacity3/refill0.5/sec admits r1–r3, rejects r4, admits r5 at 2.0. Leaky queue with 3 slots accepts r1–r3 into buffer at t≈0 and schedules departures at 2/4/6; r4 rejects; r5 can enqueue after t=2 departure, scheduled t=8. Queue acceptance is not completion.
- Boundary burst: fixed accepts three requests in [0,6) and three in [6,12), six within <1 rolling second; exact rolling limiter rejects the latter three while earlier admissions remain. Two independent G1/G2 counters each allowing 3 admits 6 under one global tenant identity.
- Serialize per-tenant/operation-class admission in an atomic authority; check stable original key/input first, then quota and persist an accepted runnable command or recoverable pending intent. A same-key retry returns its receipt with no second charge. If Redis quota state and SQL command state differ, do not claim one Lua script makes the entire operation atomic; define failure/reconciliation.
- On quota-store loss, fail closed for a hard cap or spend preallocated nonoverlapping quotas with a real epoch/revocation policy. Fail open only if the product explicitly tolerates global overshoot. Reject reuse of G1's uncertain remaining lease when it could still operate.
- Success metric for accepted work counts every original valid request and client receipt deadline, with rejections/failures/late work not counted as admitted success; quota safety separately inspects authoritative accepted originals in rolling windows. Test 2-gateway concurrency, fixed boundary, lost reply and retry, store partition with stale lease, clock rollback, and crash between quota check and job persistence.
