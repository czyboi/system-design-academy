# M11-L09 assessment — Durable KV and disposable cache

## Knowledge checks

**Q1 (LO1).** B expires at t=10 but its bytes remain allocated until t=12. A GET(B) at t=11 returns what?

**Answer.** A miss. TTL defines logical visibility independently of the moment physical memory is reclaimed; returning expired B because bytes remain would violate the declared policy.

**Q2 (LO1).** A cache at capacity removes D before its TTL expires. Is it an expiry event, and how should the app recover?

**Answer.** It is capacity eviction of a still-valid entry. The application treats the next read as a cache miss and refetches from its authoritative source or safely recomputes D; eviction must not erase an irreplaceable business fact.

**Q3 (LO1).** On replacement of a whole Memcached-like node, what failure can naive concurrent refills cause?

**Answer.** A thundering herd sends many misses to the authoritative datastore. Apply coalescing, bounded refills, jitter and graceful overload behavior while keeping the authority intact.

**Q4 (LO2).** A Redis-like service returns SET OK with AOF every-second syncing, then loses power before sync. Does the acknowledgment prove a crash-durable write?

**Answer.** No. The chosen sync policy can lose recent acknowledged writes on abrupt failure. A zero-loss promise needs a stronger actual commit/ack boundary, not merely an AOF file present on disk.

**Q5 (LO2).** Database state is v2; an old reader previously fetched v1 and puts it into the cache after the writer invalidates v1. Why is invalidate-after-write insufficient?

**Answer.** The stale reader races and repopulates v1 after invalidation. Use a version-aware install/acceptance policy or explicitly bounded staleness and TTL; the DB write and cache update are separate operations.

**Q6 (LO2).** A primary acknowledges K=v2, crashes, and a lagging replica promotes with K=v1. Can a service promising host-loss zero-data-loss treat this as success?

**Answer.** No. Its failover acknowledgment guarantee was violated. It needs a replicated committed-prefix boundary before durable acknowledgment, appropriate fencing, and reconciliation; asynchronous replica presence alone is not proof.

## EX-1 — Same interface, incompatible promises

**Scenario.** A service has `profile:42` in a database, cached on two nodes with TTL 10 seconds, and separately owns `session:42` only in a Redis-like key-value tier. The cache faces early memory eviction, a stale refill after invalidation, and a complete node restart. The key-value tier acknowledges K=v2 just before process crash, using either RDB-only, AOF everysec or a deliberately stronger synchronized/replicated commitment policy. A promoted replica may lag behind a successful response.

**Task.** Draw routing and owner responsibilities; distinguish logical expiry, physical reclaim and pressure eviction. Trace cache miss/refill with concurrent writer, full cache loss and origin overload. For each persistence policy, say exactly what acknowledged data may disappear and why, give one loss-tolerant and one zero-loss design, and define an original-request success SLI including stale, shed and timed-out operations. Offer five falsification tests.

### Reference solution

- Key-value routing uses a versioned partition map, authoritative owner/epoch, key/revision/expiry and a declared read consistency level. Clients cannot derive zero-loss durability from `SET OK` without the actual persistence/replication boundary.
- Expired B must miss after deadline even before physical sweep. Under pressure a not-yet-expired D can be evicted if it is replaceable. Cache-node loss triggers controlled refill from the database; coalescing and load limits prevent origin overload.
- DB v2 can be overwritten in cache by old in-flight v1 reader after invalidate; conditional install against a source revision or explicitly bounded staleness corrects the claim.
- RDB-only allows loss since the last snapshot; AOF everysec may lose recent acknowledged writes. Sync-before-ack with appropriate failure-domain replication can meet a stricter declared failure class if actual committed prefix/fencing is verified. No configuration automatically protects from every disaster.
- Original timely success counts all 10,000 valid reads in the lesson example; 9,100 correct timely replies yield 91%, and stale, timeout or shed originals remain in denominator.
- Falsify with TTL logical miss before reclaim, eviction before TTL, crash-before-fsync, failover-to-lagging-replica, invalidate/refill race, and cold-cache origin overload.
