# Single-leader and multi-writer replication

Profile reads dominate traffic, but a user who just changed their address expects the next profile read to show it. A second region wants to accept edits during a network break. These requirements constrain where writes are acknowledged, which copy can answer a read and who resolves conflicting edits.

- **M04-L02-LO1.** Trace log delivery, replay, promotion and concurrent branches of a replicated record.
- **M04-L02-LO2.** Choose a write authority, acknowledgement policy, read route and conflict rule for a stated failure contract.

## Follow the log to a reader

In a single-leader design, the leader runs transactions and writes their changes to a write-ahead log. A standby receives the log, makes it durable according to its configuration and replays it. A read from a new snapshot on a hot standby reflects what that standby has applied. Transport lag and replay lag are separate. A busy standby can receive changes faster than it applies them. [PostgreSQL 18 streaming replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html#STREAMING-REPLICATION), [hot standby](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/hot-standby.html).

| Acknowledgement contract | A read immediately after a successful write |
| --- | --- |
| Leader acknowledges after local durable commit, with asynchronous shipping | A follower may be behind on receipt or replay. Serve a fresh snapshot on the healthy leader or wait for evidence that an eligible follower has applied the change. |
| PostgreSQL 18 with named synchronous standbys and `synchronous_commit = on` | The required standbys have flushed WAL. A given standby can still be behind on replay. |
| PostgreSQL 18 with named synchronous standbys and `remote_apply` | The standbys required for that acknowledgement have applied the transaction. An arbitrary follower or an older read snapshot can still miss it. |

An application can build a router that carries a session watermark. Return a token representing a confirmed write position and its log history. For a later read, confirm that the chosen node has applied that position on the same history or a descendant that includes it, then open a fresh read snapshot. A position from an unrelated timeline gives no such evidence. If the deadline expires without an eligible node, use the API's explicit unavailable outcome. Ordinary reads can use an independently defined freshness policy. [PostgreSQL 18 hot standby](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/hot-standby.html), [recovery timelines](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/continuous-archiving.html#BACKUP-TIMELINES).

## Promote one writer and account for lost history

Suppose the old leader acknowledged profile changes through log position 120. The candidate has durable WAL through 118 and has replayed through 115. Assume the old leader and any source for positions 119–120 are lost. Recovering and promoting the candidate can incorporate 116–118. A read on the new leader still misses the acknowledged edit at 120. These numbers describe the actual failure history for the example. An operator with a disconnected leader may know only the candidate's position and cannot infer the unknown acknowledgement frontier from that position or an earlier lag sample.

Before opening the new writer, revoke the old leader's ability to accept writes, such as by verified isolation or stopping it through an independent control path. Then promote the eligible candidate and update routes. Rejoin the old node from the new history after checking divergence. Routing alone can leave existing connections writing to the old leader. Choose a candidate whose durable history satisfies the acknowledgement promise, or keep strict operations unavailable. A timed-out write can have an unknown result. Resolve or retry through a durable operation identity when the recovered owner can establish its state. [PostgreSQL 18 failover](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby-failover.html), [synchronous replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html#SYNCHRONOUS-REPLICATION).

For a concrete stronger contract, assume east is the active writer and west is the **fixed designated standby** in a different region. Every username claim and its unique operation ID commit atomically in the same database transaction. Configure east to wait for west's durable WAL flush with a fixed synchronous standby policy. Enforce that policy for every claim and do not silently substitute another candidate. A successful response requires proof that west has flushed the commit on the original history. This protection costs availability when west or the link is unavailable. [PostgreSQL 18 synchronous replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html#SYNCHRONOUS-REPLICATION), [synchronous commit](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/runtime-config-wal.html#GUC-SYNCHRONOUS-COMMIT).

If the service rejects a request before admitting it to a transaction, it can report a definite rejection. After admission, a lost reply or interrupted synchronous wait can leave the transaction committed locally, even though its caller has no success response. On a retry at east, finding the operation ID alone proves neither remote flush nor permission to return success. Read the committed record and obtain a WAL position on its original timeline at or after that commit. Verify from the designated west standby's current flush progress on that same history that the position is durable before returning a deduplicated success or successful status. Until that proof is available, report an unknown or pending outcome. PostgreSQL's synchronous wait can be interrupted after local commit. [PostgreSQL 18 replication wait implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/postgres/postgres/REL_18_STABLE/src/backend/replication/syncrep.c#L274-L310).

If east is lost, independently verify that east is fenced, identify west as the standby protected by the fixed policy, recover its durable WAL and promote it. The policy establishes that prior confirmed claims are in that recovered history. A recovered operation record establishes that operation's presence, while a missing record cannot prove a timed-out operation never committed on the lost east. After replay, west can serve eligible reads. With no protected standby in another region, keep new claim confirmations unavailable until that protection is restored. A recovered record for an unknown request can be reported as recovered and pending rather than newly confirmed while protection is absent. Failover decisions use the actual standby identity, WAL history and durable progress available to the operator, not a retrospective assumption about the final acknowledged position.

## Reconcile concurrent writers

Suppose east and west start with version vector `(0,0)` for a profile. During a link failure, east changes the display name and records `(1,0)`. West changes the avatar and records `(0,1)`. Neither vector dominates the other component by component, so both versions descend from the same base and are concurrent. If the fields are independent and the system retains each edit's field intent, a resolver can combine both changes, take the componentwise maximum `(1,1)` and advance its own component when committing the merged version. If both writers change the same address, a field merge needs a conflict policy such as explicit user resolution. A deterministic last-writer rule can converge while discarding one valid edit. Causal tracking is illustrated by the original Dynamo paper. The merge policy here belongs to the profile application. [DeCandia et al., Dynamo §4.4](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/files/amazon-dynamo-sosp2007.pdf#page=6).

Keep invariants such as global username uniqueness under one coordinated claim authority. Otherwise, two disconnected writers can each promise the same name before seeing the other claim. Coordination can wait for remote communication or reject writes if the required participants are unreachable. Multi-writer designs fit independently mergeable edits or workloads that can partition ownership by key.

## Choose by workload and failure contract

Use replicas to offload eligible reads and improve recovery options. The leader still runs each write, and each follower needs network bandwidth, storage and replay capacity. Synchronous acknowledgement adds a dependency to the write path. Under peak load and during a failure drill, measure commit latency, WAL generation, receive and replay progress, version misses, and recovery time. For an acknowledged-write loss target, compare original confirmed operation IDs against recovered state. Measure read success separately, including original requests that return unavailable or miss their required version. Decide up front which endpoints can accept stale data and which must wait or fail. [PostgreSQL 18 planning for replication performance](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html#SYNCHRONOUS-REPLICATION).
