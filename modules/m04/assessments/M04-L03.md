# Companion assessment for M04-L03

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** A support platform stores invoices and tickets together. A tenant produces 60% of ticket writes. Individual ticket updates are independent and include tenant and ticket ID. Agents also query the latest tickets for the tenant. Billing and tickets can have separate ownership, but a combined dashboard must report when one side is unavailable. The team proposes splitting billing and then hashing ticket rows by tenant ID. In a test ring, existing tokens are A=10, B=40 and C=75. A new owner D joins at 25. For the hottest tenant, a router may use a fixed number of ticket buckets.

Name which part of the proposal helps each workload. Trace keys 12, 28 and 90 before and after D joins. Compare tenant-only and bucketed routing for point writes and the tenant list. Outline a migration and trace a write sent with the old epoch after cutover. Give a rollback plan once the destination has accepted a write.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

What changes in the admin dashboard when billing owns its own database?

**Answer.** It must combine independent reads or use a derived view with a specified freshness and failure contract.

### Q2

Who owns key 10 before D joins?

**Answer.** A. A token includes its own position.

### Q3

After D joins, which interval changes primary owner?

**Answer.** (10, 25] moves from B to D. Other primary intervals retain their owners.

### Q4

Does hashing tenant IDs spread a 60% tenant across physical owners?

**Answer.** No. Its one tenant key maps to one owner. Distinct ticket keys or buckets can spread independent writes.

### Q5

Which routing choice improves an ordered interval query and what might it concentrate?

**Answer.** Range partitioning preserves locality on that key and can concentrate recent writes in the newest range.

### Q6

Why require a new fenced movement for rollback after the destination accepts writes?

**Answer.** The old copy no longer has the full history. First preserve the destination's new changes, then reopen exactly one writer under a new epoch.

## Sample solution and rubric

Separating billing gives billing and ticket workloads independent capacity, with a dashboard that reports partial results explicitly. Tenant hashing leaves the hot tenant on one shard. Bucket its independent tickets by ticket ID and route known-ticket writes directly. Query the four buckets with a bounded fan-out, merge their ordered results using timestamp and ticket ID, and surface incomplete results when a required bucket fails. A shared invariant across tickets needs its own coordinated authority.

Before D joins, keys 12 and 28 go to B, and 90 wraps to A. After the join, 12 goes to D, 28 stays at B and 90 stays at A. Copy and catch up from the old writable owner, fence and drain it, apply through the final position, verify the destination and switch the directory epoch. Reject a write that uses the old epoch and make its caller refresh the route. If the destination has accepted new writes, fence it and reconcile those writes to the old side before any return.
