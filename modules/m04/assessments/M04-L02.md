# Companion assessment for M04-L02

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** A profile service has two regions. Display-name and theme edits may be reconciled when the link heals. Unique username claims must stay unique, and every confirmed claim must survive the loss of the active writer region, assuming the other region survives. During a link failure, availability may be reduced for claims. In each 15-minute failure drill, at least 95% of original read requests carrying a confirmed username version must return that version or later within 400 ms from arrival to response. Stale, late, rejected and timed-out requests count as misses, with retries folded into the original request's result.

In one drill, a writer acknowledged a username claim at position 72 with asynchronous shipping. At the link break the other region has durable log through 70 and replay through 68. Positions 71–72 are inaccessible when the active region is lost. The other region answers 93 of 100 original version-carrying reads on time with the required version. It rejects three, returns two stale results and returns two correct results late.

Trace the failed recovery and score the read outcome. Design a safer username write, failover and read path. State what happens during a link break with both nodes alive. Separately trace concurrent display-name and theme changes and name the condition under which they can be merged.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

The standby has durably received through position 80 and has replayed through 76. Can a new snapshot there already see the edit at 78?

**Answer.** No. Durable receipt makes recovery possible, while read visibility waits for replay.

### Q2

The original writer acknowledged position 72 and disappears. The promoted candidate recovers only through 70. How can a successful edit be missing?

**Answer.** The acknowledgement required only local durability. The new writer has a shorter recovered history, so its fresh read cannot include 72.

### Q3

Are `(3,1)` and `(2,2)` causally ordered? How does `(3,2)` relate to them?

**Answer.** The first two are concurrent because each is greater in one component. The last dominates both and can represent combined causal context.

### Q4

Why does moving a DNS name away from the old writer fail to fence it?

**Answer.** Existing connections or stale routes can still reach that server. The write capability must be revoked before the new writer opens.

### Q5

Would an additional read replica fix a leader saturated by stock updates and row locks?

**Answer.** It can reduce contention from eligible reads, but the leader still executes stock updates. Confirm where write time is spent before changing topology.

### Q6

Can two disconnected writers independently confirm the same globally unique username and resolve it later?

**Answer.** Reconciliation cannot preserve both promises. Coordinate the claim or reserve disjoint authority for particular names before accepting them independently.

## Sample solution and rubric

The asynchronous candidate can apply 69 and 70, but the confirmed operation at 72 is absent. The read score is 93/100, below 95%. All seven failures remain in the denominator. A latency percentile of the 93 successes would answer a different question.

For username claims, designate the other-region standby as the fixed synchronous target. The owner atomically commits the claim and unique operation ID, and every successful response requires that designated standby to flush the commit on the original history. A request rejected before admission has a definite rejection. An admitted request can commit locally even if its synchronous wait or reply is interrupted. If a retry finds its ID on the original writer, obtain a WAL position at or after its commit and verify current durable flush through that position on the designated standby and history before returning deduplicated success or successful status. Without proof, return unknown or pending.

If the active region is lost, verify fencing, identify the designated standby, recover its durable WAL and promote it. The enforced fixed policy proves prior confirmations are in that history. A recovered operation record proves its presence, but an absent record does not establish whether an unknown request committed on the lost writer. With only one region, serve eligible reads after replay and keep new claim confirmations unavailable until other-region durability is restored. A recovered unknown operation remains pending for a new confirmation while that protection is absent. With both nodes alive but the link broken, keep claims unconfirmed when the required remote flush cannot be proven.

For strict reads, carry the confirmed history and position, verify replay or recovery includes it, and open a new snapshot. If no eligible route can answer by the deadline, return unavailable and count a miss. Re-run the failure drill to measure whether the revised policy meets the target. For display name and theme, retain intent from each writer. From a shared version, independent updates create concurrent branches and a resolver can merge the two fields under their stated independence rule.

| --- | ---: | --- |
| Recovery and score | 3 | Separates receipt from replay, identifies the lost acknowledgement and computes 93% on the original cohort. |
| Write and failover | 3 | Names a fixed other-region standby, atomically records the claim and operation ID, verifies remote flush before every success including retries, and uses verified fencing and designated standby recovery. Distinguishes pre-admission rejection from unknown admitted outcomes and keeps new confirmations unavailable with only one unprotected region. |
| Read policy | 2 | Uses history-aware freshness evidence and counts unavailable, stale and late results against the target. |
| Conflict policy | 2 | Shows concurrent branches, supports merging independent fields and keeps global uniqueness coordinated. |

Accept equivalent architectures that prove the same acknowledgement survival and read semantics. Award partial credit only for mechanisms explained against the stated failures.
