# Companion assessment for M04-L04

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** A merchant portal shows open refunds by merchant. Its ledger permits refunds only up to the remaining refundable amount. A separate read model serves the portal. In a ten-minute drill, track all 200 original relevant ledger commits and observe each for five seconds after its commit. Success means the read model can return that merchant's committed version or a newer complete version within five seconds. In the drill, 194 reach it on time, three arrive late and three never arrive within the observation horizon. The target is 99%.

Compare a live join, an in-transaction summary and an asynchronous view. Choose a design for a read-heavy portal that can label stale results. Trace concurrent mutations, a relay failure after the ledger commits, and duplicate and reversed delivery of versions 12 and 13. For a rebuild, assume one comparable source cursor, an old contiguous checkpoint of 90, expired records after it, a new snapshot through 120 and an old copy that has already served a version from cursor 130. Show the cutover and restart positions. Compute the measured freshness result and state which copy controls refund eligibility.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

Which copy enforces remaining refundable amount?

**Answer.** The authoritative ledger transaction. A view can lag and is intended for display.

### Q2

A refund commits and the separate summary write fails. What does a reader see?

**Answer.** The authoritative refund exists, while the summary can return an older value until repaired.

### Q3

Version 13 is applied before a duplicate of version 12. What does the complete-snapshot projector do?

**Answer.** It keeps version 13 because the conditional update requires a greater version. The older snapshot would otherwise regress the view.

### Q4

When can an in-transaction summary be preferable?

**Answer.** When readers require the new summary at commit and the extra update cost and contention fit the source transaction budget.

### Q5

Does a healthy global lag reading prove a given merchant's view includes version 12?

**Answer.** No. Compare the actual row version or read the authority under the operation's freshness contract.

### Q6

Why start a rebuild with a snapshot and change position?

**Answer.** The position identifies which later updates must be applied. A mismatched starting position can omit committed updates or replay against the wrong state.

## Sample solution and rubric

Keep refund eligibility in the ledger. For a portal allowed to label stale data, every mutation for one merchant locks its serialization row before reading or changing contributing facts. A waiting transaction then reads the prior commit and includes its own change when it constructs the complete snapshot. Without that order, a higher version can omit an earlier refund. Commit the ledger change, version and outbox event atomically.

If the relay fails after commit, the durable outbox remains available. Retire the publication only after durable broker acknowledgement. The projector applies version 13 if newer and skips any later delivery of 12. It acknowledges its input after the durable apply or safe skip, advancing a cumulative checkpoint only through a complete contiguous prefix. Route freshness-critical reads to the ledger or report the documented stale or unavailable result.

A live join avoids the extra projection write, while an in-transaction summary makes its write and possible hot-row contention part of ledger latency. For the async design the observed freshness result is 194/200, or 97%, so it misses 99%. Keep all six misses in the denominator. The new aligned snapshot covers through cursor 120 and its retained stream starts at 121. Fence and drain the old projector, then choose a source cutover boundary covering every version it can serve, including cursor 130. For a chosen boundary 140, apply or safely skip every record 121–140, durably checkpoint that contiguous prefix, validate coverage and switch readers. Restart the new generation at 141. Checkpoint 90 neither reaches the new snapshot nor captures the old out-of-order application. Repair any gap or take a new aligned snapshot before switching.

| Criterion | Points | Full credit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Authority and design | 3 | Enforces refunds at the source and compares all three options by latency, freshness and write cost. |
| Delivery trace | 3 | Locks before contributing reads and writes, builds complete snapshots in the serialized order, pairs source and outbox atomically, distinguishes durable relay and projector acknowledgements, and handles duplicate and reverse delivery with a gap-free checkpoint. |
| Freshness | 2 | Computes 97% with the stated cohort and timing and makes a precise route or stale-result choice. |
| Repair | 2 | Uses the new snapshot at 120 and aligned continuation, fences and drains the old projector, covers its out-of-order cursor 130 through a common boundary, persists a gap-free new checkpoint and resumes at its first unapplied record. |

Award partial credit for correctly explained mechanisms with incomplete traces. Accept alternatives that meet the same constraints.
