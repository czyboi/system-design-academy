# Evidence for M00-L01 — How to learn and check readiness

Checked 2026-11-11. The sources and scope of R1–R4 and R7 were revisited on 2026-11-15. The diagnostic and study schedules are original Academy examples. The cited experiments measure retention under their experimental conditions.

| R1 | A request can take effect before its response is lost. RFC 9110 advises clients against automatically retrying a non-idempotent method unless they know its semantics are idempotent or know the original request was never applied. | IETF, RFC 9110 (2022), [§9.2.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2). | Verified. The stable key belongs to the example charge endpoint's replay contract. |
| R2 | PostgreSQL commits a transaction block's changes as a unit or cancels them on rollback. With synchronous commit, successful acknowledgement follows a local WAL flush. An asynchronous commit can be acknowledged before that flush. | PostgreSQL Global Development Group, PostgreSQL 18, [§3.4 Transactions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/tutorial-transactions.html) and [§28.4 Asynchronous Commit](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/wal-async-commit.html). | Verified for the stipulated configuration. Preventing a second charge across transactions is the example application's invariant. |
| R3 | A chained hash lookup chooses a bucket and checks candidate keys.
