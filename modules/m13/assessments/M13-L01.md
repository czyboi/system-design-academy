# M13-L01 assessment — Architecture evidence and transfer

## Knowledge checks

**Q1 (LO1).** What does the 2004 MapReduce paper actually establish, and what does it not establish about a modern 20 GiB job?

**Answer.** It describes a historical key/value map-shuffle-reduce runtime with partition, failure and scheduling mechanisms. It does not prove a 20 GiB job needs thousands of workers or a particular current benchmark or managed service.

**Q2 (LO1).** Why is a 2007 Dynamo conflict-resolution description insufficient to claim a current DynamoDB transaction guarantee?

**Answer.** Historical Dynamo is a distinct system and era. Contemporary DynamoDB APIs, consistency modes and transaction guarantees require current version-scoped AWS documentation; family naming is not a source of operational guarantees.

**Q3 (LO1).** Are ZooKeeper, Dapper and Kafka interchangeable solutions for an exclusive write owner?

**Answer.** No. ZooKeeper is coordination, Dapper is tracing, Kafka is a retained partitioned log under specific contracts. A leader also needs resource-side fencing against an old writer; tracing observations do not enforce it.

**Q4 (LO2).** A one-region immutable nightly batch meets its deadline with one scheduled worker. Which historic components may be unnecessary?

**Answer.** Distributed shuffle, worker orchestration, GFS/HDFS-like custom storage, continuous stream topology and global transaction coordination unless measurable constraints justify them. Preserve only immutable input pinning, recoverable run identity and atomic finished-output publication.

**Q5 (LO2).** A report's reducer finishes half an output then crashes. Is a task-success count adequate evidence for user success?

**Answer.** No. Publish an immutable completed attempt with a guarded final manifest, not half an output. The user SLI counts full correct reports visible by deadline across all original scheduled runs.

**Q6 (LO2).** What belongs in an architecture source ledger?

**Answer.** Primary title/publisher/date/section, original assumptions and failure model, claimed mechanism, target version and workload, evidence limits, rights/attribution and any current product facts still unverified.

## EX-1 — Adapt historical infrastructure to a smaller team

**Scenario.** A team has 20 GiB immutable daily input, one nightly correct 06:00 report deadline, 200 deterministic transforms, and one region. A teammate proposes (A) a 2004 MapReduce-style multi-worker pipeline plus GFS-like storage; another (B) a Spark-like in-memory lineage pipeline; a third (C) one bounded-memory scheduled worker reading object storage and publishing a versioned complete output. An old blog claims that a current globally distributed database makes all coordination free. Trial evidence: 27 of 30 report runs complete on time, two late, one integrity failure. A process crashes midway through output.

**Task.** Produce a source/date/section evidence ledger for at least two primary historical families, a constraint-to-component table, and a diagram for the smallest defensible plan. Trace failure and guarded final publication. Compare when alternatives A/B actually become justified, identify at least three unproven claims, include a rights-safe source plan, and calculate the original-report SLI with five falsification tests.

### Reference solution

Start with C if measurement confirms sufficient single-worker capacity: pin source version/hash and run ID, stage immutable attempt output, validate completeness, atomically select a finished report revision. On crash, retry stable run ID and deduplicate final publication, never show half output. The 2004 MapReduce paper supports partition/shuffle/task-retry mechanisms for larger jobs; GFS 2003 supports a historical large-file append-heavy design, not mandatory current object storage. Spark RDD lineage may pay off for iterative in-memory reuse, not inherently one pass. If the deadline is unattainable on a measured single worker, distribute deterministic partitions and merge under a guarded manifest. 27/30 = 90% correct on-time reports; do not use worker-attempt denominator. A historical blog or old Dynamo paper cannot guarantee current Spanner/DynamoDB/MongoDB semantics; verify current APIs/versions. Cite original paper titles, years and exact mechanism sections, link rather than reproduce figures. Falsify with mid-run crash, duplicate schedule trigger, input-version change during processing, lost final acknowledgment, checksum mismatch and workload increase.
