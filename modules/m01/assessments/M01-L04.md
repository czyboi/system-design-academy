# Assessment for Availability and failover budgets

## EX-1 · Recover a parcel-status service

North normally serves reads and is the only writer. South has a provisioned standby. At `t = 0`, North fails after acknowledging its last update. Every update acknowledged through `t = −8 s` is present in South's applied snapshot. Later acknowledged updates are absent. The read contract allows a snapshot up to 12 seconds behind the latest update acknowledged at the instant of failure. The RTO is 80 seconds to the first successful read, and the RPO allows six seconds of lost acknowledged updates. The service has a 99.99% time-based availability target over a 30-day window with no exclusions.

The hypothetical recovery proceeds as follows.

| Time from failure | Event |
| ---: | --- |
| 12 s | Heartbeat deadline expires. |
| 28 s | An independent management controller confirms North powered off and prevents restart as a writer. |
| 50 s | South is promoted. Assume the controller independently verifies the read contract. |
| 57 s | The router directs requests to South. |
| 70 s | The first read succeeds. |

**Deliverable.** Calculate restoration time, the recovery point gap and the remaining 30-day downtime budget after this incident. Evaluate both objectives and the read contract. Explain the response if fencing cannot be confirmed, and propose one change each for recovery time and data-loss exposure. Identify one assumption you would verify before claiming the standby will handle redirected traffic.

### Sample solution

- The interruption lasts 70 seconds, ten seconds inside the 80-second RTO. South's recovery point is eight seconds before the failure, two seconds outside the six-second RPO. Its snapshot is eight seconds behind the final acknowledged update and meets the modeled 12-second read contract.
- A 99.99% target over 30 days allows 259.2 seconds. After this incident, `259.2 − 70 = 189.2 s` remain, assuming no other downtime or exclusions in that window.
- If fencing is unconfirmed, keep South's writer disabled. A heartbeat timeout alone cannot exclude a still-running North writer. Serve the strict read endpoint only with verified freshness, routing and capacity. Return unavailable for that endpoint if the freshness evidence is missing.
- To improve recovery time, investigate the 12-to-28-second fencing interval and pre-provision capacity or routing while preserving verified exclusion. To reduce data-loss exposure, consider remote synchronous acknowledgment for the relevant commits and explicitly evaluate its extra latency and behavior when South is unreachable. A retained and tested backup supplies an earlier recovery path for corrupted data.
- Verify South has enough capacity for the redirected read rate and that its snapshot meets the freshness contract when it receives traffic. Those conditions, as well as independent routing and shared services, affect usable availability.

### Rubric

Award 0, 1 or 2 points for missing, partial or complete reasoning in each row. Accept other designs when their assumptions and failure trade-offs are explicit.

| Criterion | Full credit |
| --- | --- |
| Timeline and objectives | Calculates 70 seconds to recovery and an eight-second recovery point gap, and evaluates RTO, RPO and freshness separately. |
| Budget | Computes 259.2 seconds allowed in 30 days and 189.2 seconds remaining under the stated model. |
| Safety | Keeps writes disabled without verified exclusion and explains the conditions for continuing reads. |
| Decision and verification | Proposes distinct time and data-loss improvements, names the associated trade-offs, and checks survivor capacity or a shared dependency. |

## Knowledge checks

### Q1 · Follow the failover

In the lesson's East/West example, when does the measured read outage end, how long does it last, and does it meet the 45-second RTO?

**Answer.** It ends at the first successful read at `t = 60 s`, so it lasts 60 seconds and misses the RTO by 15 seconds. Detection and promotion are intermediate events, while the stated objective measures restoration of the read operation.

### Q2 · Assess the recovery point

The latest East update was acknowledged at failure time, and West recovered all updates through seven seconds before failure. What is the modeled recovery point gap, and does it satisfy a five-second RPO?

**Answer.** The gap is seven seconds, so it misses the RPO by two seconds. The example explicitly supplies the recovered point. A measurement of lag at some earlier time would need additional evidence to establish the same result.

### Q3 · Calculate a dependency model

Two independently usable regional paths each have availability 0.99 in the same window. Calculate the service availability if either path can handle the whole request. What is the result if both independent paths are required?

**Answer.** Either sufficient gives `1 − (1 − 0.99)² = 0.9999`, or 99.99%. Both required gives `0.99 × 0.99 = 0.9801`, or 98.01%. The model assumes independence, sufficient capacity and no switching interruption.

### Q4 · Respond to a lost heartbeat

The controller can no longer reach the primary over the service network, and its independent power controller cannot confirm shutdown. Its only freshness evidence is a lag measurement from before the loss of contact. Should the standby accept writes or serve the endpoint with the ten-second freshness contract?

**Answer.** Keep standby writes disabled until exclusion of the old writer is verified. The primary may still be accepting writes across a partition. Return unavailable for the ten-second contract because the earlier lag measurement cannot establish it. A separately authorized stale-read operation can use the snapshot if its own contract, capacity and routing permit it.

### Q5 · Choose a recovery mechanism

A team wants to reduce the chance that a primary failure loses an acknowledged update and also recover from an accidental deletion replicated to both regions. Which mechanisms address each need and what trade-off should the team test?

**Answer.** A suitable required remote acknowledgment can reduce exposure to primary loss for the covered failure cases. A retained, independently recoverable backup and log archive can restore a point before the deletion. Test write latency and unavailability when the remote acknowledgment is unreachable, and test the backup's recovery point and restore time.

### Q6 · Explain the shared dependency

The two independent regional paths from Q3 each remain usable with probability 0.99 when a shared identity service is up. Identity is available with probability 0.998 and is required for every read. What is the modeled overall availability, and why is the two-region 99.99% figure insufficient by itself?

**Answer.** `0.998 × [1 − (1 − 0.99)²] = 0.9979002`, or 99.79002%. Both regions depend on identity, so its failure makes both paths unusable for the operation. The conditional path assumptions and any switching time must also be justified.
