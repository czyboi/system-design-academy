# Availability and failover budgets assessment

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** An order-status service has one active region and one running standby. Its time-based target is 99.99% over exactly 30 days. Its RTO is 80 seconds and its RPO is 10 seconds. Both regions depend on one shared identity service. A rehearsal observes 15 seconds for detection, 10 seconds to fence the prior writer, 25 seconds to promote and verify data readiness, and 20 seconds for routing and a client probe. The latest recovered data point is 12 seconds before the interruption.

Draw the elapsed timeline, calculate the interruption and remaining time budget after this incident, and compare the observations with RTO and RPO. Explain why a calculation using two independent regional paths can overstate service availability. Recommend one change and a test of it.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

What is the allowed downtime for a 99.9% time-based target over exactly 30 days?

Answer: 2,592 seconds, or 43 minutes 12 seconds.

Explanation: Thirty days contain 2,592,000 seconds, and the unavailable fraction is 0.001.

### Q2

What is the modeled availability of two independent required serial dependencies, each at 99%?

Answer: 98.01%.

Explanation: Both must work, giving `0.99 × 0.99 = 0.9801` under the stated independence assumption.

### Q3

In the lesson's failover timeline, how long is the interruption and what condition ends it?

Answer: 65 seconds, ending when routing has shifted and a client probe confirms useful reads.

Explanation: Detection, fencing, readiness and routing occur before client-observed restoration.

### Q4

Why can a missing heartbeat be insufficient to promote a second writer safely?

Answer: It can indicate a failed network path while the old writer still runs.

Explanation: Fencing prevents the former writer from continuing to accept writes before the standby receives authority, reducing split-brain risk.

### Q5

Why can two 99% standby paths fail to achieve the independent parallel estimate of 99.99%?

Answer: Their failures can share a cause, or the surviving path may lack working routing, capacity or sufficiently current data.

Explanation: The estimate assumes independent path states and that either path can actually satisfy the operation.

### Q6

A mistaken deletion is replicated immediately to the standby. What recovery capability would help restore an earlier state?

Answer: A retained backup that covers the affected data and a tested restore process.

Explanation: Replication has carried the deletion to the standby. Recovery depends on the backup's coverage, retention and successful restoration.

## Answer key / rubric

The timeline reaches detection at 15 seconds, fencing at 25 seconds, readiness at 50 seconds and confirmed client recovery at 70 seconds. The 70-second interruption meets the 80-second RTO by 10 seconds. The recovered data point is 12 seconds old relative to disruption, exceeding the 10-second RPO by 2 seconds. The 30-day time budget is 259.2 seconds, leaving 189.2 seconds after this incident if the whole interruption counts and no other downtime occurred.

Both regions need the shared identity service. Its outage can affect both paths and violates the assumption that the two paths fail independently. A defensible change improves replica freshness or acknowledgment and promotion policy to meet the RPO, then tests disruption and recovery while measuring the recovered point. Alternatives that address the shared dependency or reduce recovery time are acceptable when justified with their trade-offs.

Award up to 4 points, one each for the timeline and RTO, data point and RPO, time budget arithmetic, and shared-dependency explanation with a justified change and test.
