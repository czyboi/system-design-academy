# Capacity and latency budgets — companion assessment

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** A feed receives 432,000 events and 4,320,000 reads each day. Each stored event is 2,000 bytes, each read response is 1,000 bytes, and 30 days of events are stored in three full copies. Use a fourfold peak for both operations and add 25% to stored payload for a modeled margin. Assume each newly created event is a distinct item and a two-hour working set contains only items created at the average rate.

Calculate average and peak reads and writes per second, replicated retained storage plus margin, peak read payload egress in Mbit/s and raw working-set bytes. The request path has two dependent remote calls assumed to take 3 ms and 20 ms. Propose one allocation or path change, state its expected effect and identify two measurements that could change the decision.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

The feed creates 864,000 events daily. What is the average event rate over a uniform day?

Answer: 10 events/s.

Explanation: A day has 86,400 seconds, so 864,000 ÷ 86,400 = 10.

### Q2

A 30-day estimate includes 1,000 bytes per event and one full copy. Which multiplier accounts for three full copies?

Answer: Multiply by three.

Explanation: The copy count includes the primary and the two replicas. Retention is a separate multiplier.

### Q3

At 400 responses/s and 2,000 bytes per response, what is the payload egress in Mbit/s?

Answer: 6.4 Mbit/s.

Explanation: 400 × 2,000 × 8 = 6,400,000 bits/s.

### Q4

An estimate adds 25% to 100 GB of payload. How does that differ from reserving 25% of provisioned capacity?

Answer: Adding 25% gives 125 GB. Reserving 25% requires approximately 133.3 GB.

Explanation: In the second case, 100 GB is 75% of provisioned capacity, so divide by 0.75.

### Q5

Two calls take 1 ms and 40 ms in a trace. The second requires the first call's result. Which change could reduce their contribution to the critical path?

Answer: Remove the second dependency, or redesign the calls to allow a correct batch or overlap.

Explanation: While the calls remain dependent their observed intervals add. The new design must still return the required data and be measured end to end.

### Q6

Why should a team measure a deployment even if a historical table provides storage and network times?

Answer: Actual devices, networks, access patterns and queueing may differ from the table.

Explanation: Historical values illustrate relative scales. They cannot establish the present request's latency distribution or capacity.

## Answer key / rubric

The sample model yields 5 average writes/s and 50 average reads/s. The fourfold peak is 20 writes/s and 200 reads/s. Retained payload is 432,000 × 30 × 2,000 × 3 = 77.76 GB, and 25% added gives 97.2 GB. Peak read egress is 200 × 1,000 × 8 = 1.6 Mbit/s. The raw working set is 5 × 7,200 × 2,000 = 72,000,000 bytes. The two dependent calls contribute 23 ms to the modeled trace before other work.

An acceptable choice is to batch the second lookup when its inputs permit it, then compare end-to-end latency and error rate under peak traffic. Another is to provision storage above 97.2 GB after estimating backups and physical overhead, and measure actual physical bytes and growth. Either answer should identify an assumption and a failure signal.

Award 10 points: 3 for rates, 2 for retention and copies, 2 for egress and working set, 1 for serial latency, and 2 for a justified choice with two measurements. Accept equivalent units and alternatives that preserve the stated requirements.
