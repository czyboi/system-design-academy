# Assessment for Capacity and latency budgets

## EX-1 · Size a 30-day news activity feed

A news feed creates 432,000 records per day and serves 4.32 million feed reads per day. Each record has a 2,000-byte stored payload. Each feed read sends 1,000 payload bytes to the client. One event is one API write, and one feed read is one API read. Use a 30-day month, 86,400 seconds per day, and a peak rate four times the daily average for both operations. The retention window is full and each record expires completely after 30 days.

The data has three full copies. Indexes and metadata add 20% to the three-copy payload. Provision a further 25% of the subtotal as margin. For a separate normal-traffic one-hour interval, assume every record created during the hour is accessed at least once and no older record is accessed. Exclude cache metadata. The design budget is 120 GB of provisioned storage and 2 Mbit/s of API-to-client read payload at peak. All values are hypothetical, and GB and Mbit use powers of ten.

**Deliverable.** Estimate average and peak request rates, 30-day logical payload, three-copy payload and provisioned storage, peak API-to-client read payload bandwidth and the distinct working set in the one-hour interval. Evaluate the two given budgets and identify two assumptions to measure before committing to the design. State a plausible approach if the high case exceeds either budget.

### Sample solution

- Average writes are `432,000 / 86,400 = 5 requests/s` and average reads are `4,320,000 / 86,400 = 50 requests/s`. Peak rates are 20 writes/s and 200 reads/s. Thirty days create 12.96 million records and 129.6 million reads.
- Logical retained payload is `12.96 million × 2,000 B = 25.92 GB`. Three copies require `25.92 × 3 = 77.76 GB` before overhead. With indexes and metadata, then the stated margin, provision `77.76 × 1.20 × 1.25 = 116.64 GB`. This fits the 120 GB budget by 3.36 GB under the assumptions.
- Peak read payload at the API-to-client interface is `200 × 1,000 × 8 = 1,600,000 bit/s`, or 1.6 Mbit/s. It fits the payload budget by 0.4 Mbit/s. Headers and retransmissions are outside the stated quantity and need separate measurement for actual link capacity.
- The distinct one-hour set is `5 × 3,600 × 2,000 = 36,000,000 B`, or 36 MB of payload. Additional reads of those same records raise read traffic without changing the distinct set. Older record accesses would increase it.
- Measure peak multiplier and payload-size distribution, then repeat with plausible higher values and the actual internal call and replication pattern. A high case that exceeds storage might justify a larger allocation or an agreed change to retention. For read bandwidth, larger link capacity or a smaller response are candidates, subject to the service contract and a measured load test.

### Rubric

Award 0, 1 or 2 points per row for missing, partial or complete reasoning. Accept other defensible changes with explicit assumptions and units.

| Criterion | Full credit |
| --- | --- |
| Rates and interval | Converts both daily counts to per-second averages and applies the stated peak and 30-day interval. |
| Storage | Includes retention, all full copies, the distinct overhead and margin factors, and compares the result with the budget. |
| Network and working set | Uses bytes-to-bits at the named interface and counts distinct records over the stated interval. |
| Decision and measurement | Checks both budgets, identifies assumptions to measure and justifies an alternative for an unfavorable high case. |

## Knowledge checks

### Q1 · Time windows

The lesson's feed receives 8.64 million reads per day. What are the average reads/s and the modeled peak at a fourfold multiplier? Why does the monthly total alone fail to specify peak rate?

**Answer.** `8,640,000 / 86,400 = 100 reads/s` on average and `100 × 4 = 400 reads/s` at peak. The monthly total does not specify how those reads are distributed within the month or within a day.

### Q2 · Bytes and binary prefixes

An estimate is 1 GiB. How many bytes is that, and why does treating it as 1 GB change the result?

**Answer.** `1 GiB = 2^30 = 1,073,741,824 B`. A decimal GB is `10^9 B`. The units use different multipliers, so treating them as equal understates the GiB quantity by 73,741,824 bytes.

### Q3 · Find the missing factor

The lesson's feed creates 864,000 events/day, stores each for 30 days at 1,000 B, and maintains three full copies. A proposal estimates `864,000 × 1,000 = 864,000,000 B` for the full three-copy retained payload. Which factors are missing, and what is the result before overhead?

**Answer.** Multiply the one-day payload by 30 retention days and three copies. `864,000 × 1,000 × 30 × 3 = 77,760,000,000 B`, or 77.76 GB. Overhead and margin would be added separately.

### Q4 · Latency decision

In the lesson's hypothetical read trace, a 12 ms lookup and an 8 ms lookup appear between 5 ms of initial path work and a 3 ms response path. What changes if the lookups are independent and overlap? What needs checking before choosing that design?

**Answer.** Sequential execution takes `5 + 12 + 8 + 3 = 28 ms`. Perfect overlap takes `5 + max(12, 8) + 3 = 20 ms`. The lookups must have no data dependency and enough concurrency. Measure the extra resource and connection demand as well as end-to-end latency under load.

### Q5 · A working-set assumption

The lesson estimates 72 MB for two hours of normal feed traffic. A new trace shows users also read distinct older events during that interval. Is the estimate still the full distinct payload working set?

**Answer.** It is incomplete. The 72 MB model counted all events created during the two hours and assumed no older event was touched. Count distinct additional older records and their payload sizes. Repeated reads of a record already counted do not add another distinct record.

### Q6 · Identify the network boundary

At the lesson's peak, 40 event writes/s have 1,000-byte payloads. Each primary write sends the payload once to each of two additional replicas. Estimate the primary's outgoing replication payload in Mbit/s. Does this include client reads or protocol overhead?

**Answer.** `40 × 1,000 × 8 × 2 = 640,000 bit/s`, or 0.64 Mbit/s. This is the assumed primary-to-replica payload only. Client reads, headers, retransmissions and other internal messages need their own budgets.
