# Evidence for M01-L04 — Availability and failover budgets

Checked 2026-11-12 against the approved [outline](../../../curriculum/revision-1/CURRICULUM_MASTER.md#m01-l04). The two-region timeline, thresholds and probabilities are hypothetical.

| ID | Claim and boundary | Exact primary source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AV-01 | Active-passive sends ordinary traffic to one site. Cold capacity takes time to start, provision or restore. Warm capacity runs at reduced scale. Hot capacity is prepared for target traffic, subject to health and freshness. Active-active serves from both sites and requires survivor capacity, routing and valid data semantics. Traffic roles and writer roles are separate. | AWS, [Disaster recovery options, Pilot light, Warm standby and Multi-site active/active](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/disaster-recovery-workloads-on-aws/disaster-recovery-options-in-the-cloud.html). The lesson defines its capacity terms explicitly. PostgreSQL 18 uses [hot standby, §26.4.1](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/hot-standby.html) for read-only queries during recovery, where lag is possible. | Verified |
| AV-02 | Missed heartbeats indicate lost contact and can reflect partition. Verify that the old writer cannot write before enabling a new one. The model uses an independently reachable power controller with verified shutdown and inhibits old-primary restart until safe rejoin. With unverified exclusion, the standby keeps writes disabled. | PostgreSQL 18, [§26.3 Failover](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby-failover.html). Red Hat Enterprise Linux 10 HA guide, [Chapter 9, Configuring fencing](https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_enterprise_linux/10/html/configuring_and_managing_high_availability_clusters/configuring-fencing), introduction and §9.12.5. Timing and controller availability are assumptions. | Verified |
| AV-03 | Asynchronous commit can acknowledge writes that have yet to reach a surviving replica. Required synchronous remote acknowledgment reduces exposure under the stated failure and configuration assumptions. Durable receipt and query visibility are distinct. Restorable point-in-time backups provide a recovery path after a bad change propagates to replicas. | PostgreSQL 18, [§26.2.5 and §26.2.8, Streaming and Synchronous Replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html#SYNCHRONOUS-REPLICATION) and [§25.3, Continuous Archiving and Point-in-Time Recovery](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/continuous-archiving.html). Acknowledge and replay behavior depends on configuration and eligible standbys. | Verified |
| AV-04 | RTO is acceptable time to restoration. RPO is acceptable data loss measured in time relative to the interruption. Evaluate actual restoration time and the latest usable recovery point separately. | AWS Well-Architected Framework, [REL13-BP01 Define recovery objectives](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/reliability-pillar/rel_planning_for_recovery_objective_defined_recovery.html). A modeled recovery point is an input. Generic observed lag alone cannot prove a loss bound. | Verified |
| AV-05 | A time-based availability target permits `(1 − target) × window` downtime under a binary definition of service and stated exclusions. Over 30 days, 99.9% allows 2,592 seconds and 99.99% allows 259.2 seconds. A request SLI has a separate denominator of eligible requests. | Google, *Site Reliability Engineering* (2016), [Appendix A](https://sre.google/sre-book/availability-table/) and [Embracing Risk, Measuring Service Risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/#measuring-service-risk). Values above use unrounded arithmetic. | Verified |
| AV-06 | With independent same-window up/down events, both required gives `A × B`, while either sufficient gives `1 − (1 − A)(1 − B)`. Count a branch as usable only with enough capacity, valid data and working routing. Common causes require joint or conditional probabilities. A hard shared service gives `P(shared up) × P(either usable given shared up)`. | AWS, *Availability and Beyond*, [Availability with dependencies, Eq. 4](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/availability-and-beyond-improving-resilience/availability-with-dependencies.html) and [Availability with redundancy, Eq. 5](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/availability-and-beyond-improving-resilience/availability-with-redundancy.html). Independence and switch overhead depend on the scenario. | Verified |
