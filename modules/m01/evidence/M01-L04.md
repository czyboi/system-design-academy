# Evidence for M01-L04 — Availability and failover budgets

Checked 2026-11-11. The two-region service, timings and dependency probabilities are original hypothetical inputs.

| ID | Claim | Publisher, version and section | Scope and confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Active-passive moves traffic from the active site to standby. Ready capacity can omit startup and scale-out from the recovery path. Cold capacity may need provisioning, startup and restore. Active-active serves both sites and requires healthy endpoints with surviving capacity. | AWS, *Disaster Recovery of Workloads on AWS*, [Disaster recovery options in the cloud](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/disaster-recovery-workloads-on-aws/disaster-recovery-options-in-the-cloud.html), backup and restore, pilot light, warm standby and multi-site active/active sections. | Verified for these patterns. Specify compute, data and traffic readiness. Read and write traffic roles can differ. |
| A2 | A lost heartbeat can signal a broken link. Fence the prior primary before promoting a replacement to write authority. Confirm readiness and verify traffic recovery. | PostgreSQL Global Development Group, PostgreSQL 18, [§26.3 Failover](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby-failover.html), heartbeat, witness, STONITH and promotion. | Verified. PostgreSQL 18 supplies promotion commands and relies on external detection and orchestration. Read-only routing can change without promotion if its service contract holds. |
| A3 | Replication carries changes, including accidental deletions. Asynchronous promotion can miss acknowledged writes. Multi-writer systems need a policy for concurrent writes. Backups and tested restores can recover an earlier clean state within retention. | PostgreSQL 18, [§26.1 Comparison of Different Solutions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/different-replication-solutions.html), [§26.2.5 and §26.2.8](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html). AWS whitepaper above, Backup and restore and Multi-site active/active. | Verified. Scope write durability to acknowledgment and promotion rules. PostgreSQL physical replication here is single-writer. |
| A4 | Recovery time objective limits interruption duration. Recovery point objective bounds the tolerated time gap to the recovered state. | AWS Well-Architected Framework (2024-06-27), [REL13-BP01 Define recovery objectives](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/2024-06-27/framework/rel_planning_for_recovery_objective_defined_recovery.html), definitions and implementation guidance. | Verified. Recovery targets and observed outcomes are separate measures. |
| A5 | A time-based availability target A over T allows (1 − A) × T downtime. For exactly 30 days, 99.9% allows 43 min 12 s and 99.99% allows 4 min 19.2 s. | Google, *Site Reliability Engineering* (2016), [Appendix A, Table of Availability](https://sre.google/sre-book/availability-table/), aggregate unavailability and three/four nines rows. | Verified, with direct arithmetic for the stated window. Request-based availability uses bad eligible requests as its unit. |
| A6 | Serial success needs both dependencies to work. Either-suffices success is the complement of both down. Independence permits multiplication of the corresponding probabilities. | AWS, *Availability and Beyond*, [Availability with dependencies, eq. 4](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/availability-and-beyond-improving-resilience/availability-with-dependencies.html) and [Availability with redundancy, eq. 5](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/availability-and-beyond-improving-resilience/availability-with-redundancy.html), web whitepaper. | Verified. Shared dependencies, health detection, routing, freshness and surviving capacity affect actual success. |
