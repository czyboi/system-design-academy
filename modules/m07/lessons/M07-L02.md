# Back pressure, bounded work and retry control

A thumbnail service usually keeps up with incoming work. A product launch doubles its arrivals for several seconds. An acceptance response creates an obligation, so the service needs a limit on how much deadline-sensitive work it can still promise.

- **M07-L02-LO1.** Model backlog growth, drain time and a waiting-time budget for a fixed-rate worker pool.
- **M07-L02-LO2.** Choose admission, scaling and retry controls that protect both capacity and timely completion.

## Set the boundary for queue math

Track offered original jobs, admitted jobs, retry attempts, oldest waiting age, in-flight work and completion rate separately. With a stable workload and compatible long-run averages, Little's law gives `L = λW`. If `L` counts only waiting jobs, use their arrival rate and wait until service begins. If it includes jobs in service, use total residence time. For example, 8 waiting jobs on average and 20 admitted jobs/s in a steady regime imply a mean wait of 0.4 s when all admitted jobs pass through this boundary and leave it to start service. The mean supplies neither a percentile nor a deadline for the next job. [Little, Operations Research 1961](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.3.383).

## Trace one burst and its recovery

Use a fluid approximation with homogeneous jobs, FIFO service and all worker slots busy at the burst start. The fleet can start 40 jobs/s while work is waiting. Each started job takes 1 s, and dispatch is immediate. With a 3 s completion deadline, allocate at most 2 s to waiting. This allows 80 jobs of waiting work in the model. Start with zero waiting. During a 4 s burst at 70 jobs/s, unconstrained waiting work grows by `(70 − 40) × 4 = 120` jobs and the final wait reaches 3 s. With a cap at 80, it fills at 80/30 ≈ 2.67 s, then rejects 30 jobs/s for the remainder. That rejects 40 of 280 originals. If load then drops to 25 jobs/s, the 80 remaining jobs drain in `80/(40 − 25) ≈ 5.33 s`. If arrivals stay at 40, the inventory stays full. If they exceed 40, the limit keeps excess work out.

For the cohort of originals arriving during the four-second burst, time each request from initial arrival to usable completion, observing the full three seconds for the last arrivals as well. With the stated ideal timing, all 240 admitted jobs finish by their deadlines, but only 240/280 ≈ 85.7% of originals succeed on time. A 95% user target fails even though admitted-only latency passes. Count rejected, failed and late originals as misses. At a 95% target, at most 14 jobs may miss in this model. Keeping the cap at 80 and finding more verified service capacity can still meet the user target. Under the same assumptions the fleet would need to start at least `(266 − 80)/4 = 46.5` jobs/s during the burst. Check the waiting budget again at the new rate. In a real pool with variable durations and dispatch delay, use measured work estimates and age/deadline signals. A fixed job count alone cannot enforce a time budget.

## Put back pressure where it can work

Pace a producer that can slow down, rate-limit client groups before they overload shared work, and reject new work before accepting it if its predicted wait exceeds the budget. A bounded pending reservation keeps concurrent admissions from exceeding the cap. Release reservations on terminal outcomes, and count accepted work across the outbox, broker and delayed retries so it cannot hide outside the limit. A worker-concurrency cap protects in-flight resources, while an admission rate and burst allowance control starts over time. Shed low-priority previews first when callers have agreed to distinct service classes. Report rejection separately so a fast error cannot improve timely completion. If the downstream dependency is saturated, more workers can raise contention and make queueing worse. [Google SRE, Handling Overload](https://sre.google/sre-book/handling-overload/), [M07-L01](M07-L01.md).

## Keep retries inside the budget

Retry transient, idempotent work at one responsible layer. Carry the original job ID and deadline. For attempt index `i = 0, 1, …`, use a random delay from zero through `min(cap, base × 2^i)`. With base 200 ms and cap 800 ms, the first three ceilings are 200, 400 and 800 ms. The randomization spreads demands that failed together. Retry only when a separate rate budget permits and delay plus estimated service fits the remaining deadline. A retry consumes the same worker and downstream budgets as fresh work. If each of two layers permits three total attempts, one original can trigger nine downstream attempts. Stop on permanent errors. On overload, lower retry and admission pressure quickly, then increase gradually as queue age and outcomes recover. [AWS Builders' Library, Timeouts, retries, and backoff with jitter](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter/).
