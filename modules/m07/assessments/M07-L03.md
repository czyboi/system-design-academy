# Companion assessment for M07-L03

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** A marketplace index saved a snapshot paired with next positions P0:12 and P1:8. All non-delete events are complete searchable replacements: P0:12 updates L to revision 4, P0:13 repeats it, P0:14 changes M to revision 2, and P1:8 updates K to revision 9. P0:15 deletes L at revision 5. The snapshot is restored into a separate generation before the indexer crashes after applying P0:12 but before saving progress. An alerting group is already past these positions. Assume a retained delete-only Kafka 4.2 topic and a relay that can append revisions out of source order. Trace this generation’s replay and checkpointing, state the fenced cutover condition and explain how you would recover if P0's earliest retained offset moves to 15 before replay starts. Defend a log or queue for index updates and say how one hot listing affects parallelism.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

Does an alerting group's progress advance the search group's position?

**Answer.** No. Each group tracks its own progress in the retained log.

### Q2

What is the next position after applying contiguous records P0:12 through P0:15?

**Answer.** P0:16. P1 is tracked separately.

### Q3

A delayed replay sends an older full value for a deleted listing. What stops resurrection?

**Answer.** Keep the deletion revision marker, and reject an older revision when applying the replay.

### Q4

Would committing a position before writing the index avoid duplicates safely?

**Answer.** A crash between them can skip the missing effect. Apply durably before advancing and make repeats safe, or commit effect and position together.

### Q5

Can an extra consumer process the busiest listing's ordered partition in parallel without changing the contract?

**Answer.** Another group member can process another assigned partition. The single ordered key still needs serialization or an application-specific coordinated ordering scheme.

### Q6

Can the rebuild always start at the log's earliest retained offset if its saved cursor is gone?

**Answer.** No. Missing records can invalidate the snapshot continuation. Record and protect consumer-readable partition ends before opening a consistent authority snapshot, including deletion revision markers. Rebuild from it and replay from that earlier vector, then verify the fenced cutover barrier; overlapping full values are revision-guarded.

## Sample solution and rubric

Restore the snapshot into its own generation before the crash. Disable auto-commit and seek to that generation’s saved next positions after assignment or restart; fence workers on assignment loss. Repeat P0:12 after the crash, where its durable revision 4 makes the repeat harmless. Apply the duplicate as a no-op, M’s replacement, L’s deletion marker and K independently. Advance to P0:16 and P1:9 only after all required visible effects are durable. Numeric offset holes alone do not prove a gap; check the saved position against log start throughout, with `auto.offset.reset=none`.

Fence old writers and settle in-flight effects, then capture a consumer-readable log-end barrier covering all previously served effects, assuming they all came from the log. Cut over only when the replacement has durably processed required records through that barrier and passed checks. If P0:12 is no longer retained, starting at 15 leaves M stale. Instead protect a new partition-end vector before opening a consistent authority snapshot with revision markers for deletions, rebuild from it, and replay from that earlier vector through a validated cutover. Committed-only relay publication and retained overlap cover the gap; older delayed full values cannot undo snapshot revisions.

Use a retained delete-only log for independent groups and replay, with its retention and hot-key costs. An out-of-order compacted log is unsafe: a revision 6 delete followed by delayed revision 5 can leave 5 after compaction. A separate compacted state topic needs source-ordered publication including retries or stale-version filtering before publication, plus sufficient tombstone retention. A relay retires intent only on confirmed broker acknowledgement; uncertain sends retry the same event ID. A work queue suits one-time per-item execution instead.

| Criterion | Points | Full credit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Replay and failure | 4 | Traces both partitions, generation-owned positions, restart and fenced partition ownership, durable effects before checkpoint, deletion marker and next positions. |
| Repair and cutover | 3 | Detects missing history; establishes and protects the pre-snapshot end vector, consistent baseline and deletion markers, overlap replay, and a fenced served-effect barrier before cutover; accepts equivalent proven coverage. |
| Messaging choice | 3 | Explains independent progress, delete-only retention versus unsafe out-of-order compaction, and ordering or hot-key cost. |

Accept alternatives that satisfy the same constraints. Award partial credit for a correct trace with an incomplete justification.
