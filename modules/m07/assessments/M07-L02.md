# Companion assessment for M07-L02

## Practical exercise

**EX-1.** Assume homogeneous FIFO watermark jobs in a fluid model. A saturated pool starts 30 jobs/s while work waits and each started job immediately runs for 1 s. Initially none waits and all 30 service slots are busy. Assume no retries and no accepted execution failures in the numerical trace. For five seconds, 50 original jobs/s arrive, then arrivals drop to 20/s. A completion deadline of 3 s starts at first arrival. The five-second cohort target is 95% usable completions within 3 s, with one count per original and a full deadline for each. Compute the delay budget, capped burst, rejection and drain. Defend an admission or capacity change that could meet the target. Specify a retry policy with rate and deadline constraints, and explain why jitter alone cannot meet the target.

## Knowledge checks

### Q1

A stable queue has 60 waiting jobs on average and admits 30 jobs/s. What mean wait does Little's law imply if all admitted jobs flow through that queue to service?

**Answer.** 2 s at the queue boundary. It excludes service and says nothing about the next job's percentile.

### Q2

Can 60 queued jobs drain while 30/s arrive and the pool starts 30/s?

**Answer.** No net drain. Departures and arrivals balance.

### Q3

At 20/s arrivals and 30/s starts, how long does a 60-job backlog take to drain in the fluid model?

**Answer.** 6 s, since 10 jobs/s of net progress remains.

### Q4

Would twice as many workers help a storage dependency already at capacity?

**Answer.** Only if dependency capacity or per-job demand changes. More concurrency can amplify contention. Protect that dependency with bounded starts and in-flight work.

### Q5

Two request layers each allow one initial attempt and two retries. What is the maximum deepest-layer attempt count?

**Answer.** Nine, because each of three outer attempts can initiate three inner attempts.

### Q6

With a retry token, 200 ms cooldown, 300 ms estimated queue/dispatch wait, 600 ms execution and 1 s left before the original deadline, should a retry proceed?

**Answer.** No. The estimated 1.1 s already exceeds the unchanged deadline before publication time. Recheck before dispatch; an available token does not make it timely.

## Sample solution and rubric

The wait budget is 2 s and the cap is 60 waiting jobs; with 30 running this is 90 nonterminal jobs. Reserve identity, pending job and runnable intent atomically, counting each accepted original once through retries and releasing at a terminal outcome. An uncapped 100-job backlog implies 100/30 ≈ 3.33 s of waiting at the end. The cap fills after 3 s, then 40 of 250 originals are rejected in the final 2 s. All 210 admitted jobs can finish on time in the ideal model, for 84% timely success. The 60-job backlog drains in 6 s at 10 net starts per second. A candidate at 40 starts/s with the same waiting cap needs up to 100 total nonterminal slots with 40 running. It grows a 50-job backlog over the burst, waits at most 1.25 s and sheds no jobs in this model. Verify the higher rate at the actual dependency. Bound admission and use a separate retry-rate budget; all starts, including retries, share any downstream dispatch-rate limit and the in-flight cap. Preserve the original absolute deadline, estimate cooldown, queue/dispatch, execution and publication before retrying, and recheck before dispatch. A definite rejection may be retried; resolve an uncertain acceptance using the same caller/key or status before another admission. Count the eventual outcome once: a rejected attempt followed by on-time completion succeeds. Jitter spreads retries but supplies no extra capacity.

| Capacity and retry decision | 4 | Justifies a feasible capacity or workload change, names its dependency assumptions and separates admission, dispatch and concurrency controls, and bounds retries by rate and estimated end-to-end time against the unchanged deadline; resolves uncertain acceptance with the same identity. |

Accept alternatives that satisfy the same constraints. Award partial credit for a correct trace with an incomplete justification.
